# Ritual Structure — Section Rationale

Each of the 9 sections has a specific purpose. This file exists so the skill can keep its pacing right and explain the logic if a user asks why the review goes a certain way.

The flow follows a deliberate arc: **look back → reflect → process → look forward**. That order matters. Reflection without data is vague; data without reflection is sterile; processing before reflecting tends to skip the why. Looking forward before processing leaves loose ends to trip on Monday.

## Section 1 — Frame the session

**Purpose:** Set expectations and create a small ceremony around starting. "Ready when you are" beats jumping straight in.

**Why it matters:** A weekly review is a transition between modes — from the week that was to the week ahead. People skip the review when it feels like just another task. Two sentences of framing make it feel like a beat in time, not an interruption.

## Section 2 — MITs hit and missed

**Purpose:** Ground the review in actual data. What got done? What kept slipping?

**Why it matters:** Memory is unreliable about the week. People remember the salient moments (one bad meeting, one win), not the pattern. Reading the actual MITs surfaces the truth.

## Section 3 — Goal coverage

**Purpose:** Connect the week's work back to longer-term intent.

**Why it matters:** Days fill up with whatever's urgent. Without a weekly check, weeks can be 100% Work and 0% Health for months at a time without anyone noticing. The skill doesn't moralize — it just shows the map.

## Section 4 — Stuck patterns

**Purpose:** Surface what the user is repeatedly avoiding or losing momentum on.

**Why it matters:** Single unblock sessions help in the moment. Patterns across sessions reveal what's actually going on — the wrong project, the wrong role, the wrong relationship to a task. This is data the user can only see in aggregate.

## Section 5 — Reflect

**Purpose:** Generate insights the user can act on.

**Why it matters:** This is the highest-leverage section. The processing sections (6-7) clean the slate; the looking-forward sections (8-9) plan the next slate. Reflection is where the user makes meaning out of what just happened. Skip this and the review is just a TODO-list janitor.

The questions vary across weeks (`reflective-questions.md`) to prevent the same answers from being repeated rotely.

## Section 6 — next-actions.md cleanup

**Purpose:** Empty the queue of items that no longer matter.

**Why it matters:** Stale tasks in a queue are friction every time the user looks at it. Worse, they erode trust in the system — when half the list is dead, the user stops believing the live items either. One-by-one feels slow but is the only way to actually clean.

The Defer bucket exists for items that might matter later but shouldn't be in the active queue today. Better than dropping (which loses the thought) and better than keeping (which clutters).

## Section 7 — Brain dump backlog scan

**Purpose:** Pull forward Ideas, Decisions, and Questions that have been sitting in brain dumps without resolution.

**Why it matters:** The brain dump skill captures everything, which is its strength, but it doesn't naturally surface the slow-burn items. The weekly is when "I keep thinking about Decision X" becomes "let me actually decide."

Skip the Tasks bucket of dumps — those are already in next-actions. Skip Notes — they're reference.

## Section 8 — Calendar preview

**Purpose:** Notice prep needs before they become Monday-morning emergencies.

**Why it matters:** Most weekly stress comes from prep that gets pushed to the moment of need. A 60-second scan of the week ahead catches "I should prep for the Thursday board update" while there's still time to act on it.

## Section 9 — Pick next week's priorities

**Purpose:** Create a weekly compass that every daily plan reads.

**Why it matters:** Daily planning is good for tactics, not strategy. Without a weekly compass, you can have a perfectly executed week that didn't move anything important forward. Limiting to 3-5 priorities forces real choice — what does this week actually need to be about?

The priorities file is read by the Daily Plan Builder as context every day. It's a quiet but powerful integration — the user sets intent on Sunday and gets reminded of it Monday through Friday without re-typing.
