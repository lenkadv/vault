# Diagnostic Clues

When the user's answer to "what's actually going on with this task?" doesn't fit cleanly into one of the six categories, use these phrase-to-category mappings to choose.

## Overwhelm signals

User says: "too much", "where do I even start", "it's huge", "so many parts", "I keep looking at it and shutting the lid", "drowning", "buried", "overloaded"

→ **Overwhelm**

## Ambiguity signals

User says: "I don't know how to do it", "I'm not sure what they want", "the brief is vague", "what am I even supposed to do here", "I keep going in circles", "I've been thinking about it all week"

→ **Ambiguity**

## Fear / perfectionism signals

User says: "what if it's bad", "they'll judge it", "I want it to be perfect", "I keep deleting and starting over", "I'm not good enough at this", "I can already see how it won't be good", "I don't want to embarrass myself", "high-stakes"

→ **Fear / perfectionism**

## Boredom / aversion signals

User says: "I just don't want to", "it's so boring", "this is the worst part of my week", "I hate this kind of work", "ugh", "this is a slog", "I'd rather do anything else"

→ **Boredom / aversion**

## Low energy signals

User says: "I'm tired", "I'm exhausted", "brain fog", "can't focus", "I keep zoning out", "depleted", "I haven't slept well", "burned out lately", "got nothing left"

→ **Low energy**

## No-stakes signals

User says: "I'm not sure it matters", "no one's pushing for it", "I keep moving it", "self-imposed", "it's been on the list forever", "I don't even know why I'm doing this", "I won't get fired if I don't"

→ **No stakes**

## Mixed signals

Users often describe several things at once. When in doubt:

1. **If they mention a physical/energy state** (tired, hungry, burned out) AND something else, treat low energy as the primary — fix the body state first, the rest gets re-evaluated.

2. **If they mention "perfect" or "good enough" anywhere**, fear/perfectionism is almost always the real driver — even when wrapped in "overwhelm" language.

3. **If the task has been stuck 2+ weeks**, no-stakes is the likely real culprit even if they pick something else. Surface this gently.

4. **If they refuse all six categories** ("none of these fit"), default to ambiguity. The ambiguity intervention (define "done" and next concrete action) works as a low-cost diagnostic-by-doing.

## Phrases that suggest a non-procrastination issue

Sometimes what looks like procrastination is something heavier and the skill shouldn't try to fix it:

- "I haven't been able to do anything for weeks" (could be depression or burnout)
- "Every task feels like this" (chronic, not task-specific)
- "I cry when I think about it" (emotional load past what an unblock skill can address)
- "I keep wanting to disappear / can't get out of bed" (escalate to: "this sounds bigger than a stuck task")

When you see these, prescribe: "This sounds bigger than one task being stuck. Worth talking to someone you trust — a friend, doctor, or therapist. The unblock skill can help with single tasks; I don't want to skip past what you're describing." Then end the session without forcing a diagnosis.
