### Don’t Poop In The Rocks: A2

In a national park, there was a strange problem. The park was in the mountains and was very beautiful. Many people visited the park to see the rocks and walk on the trails. But with more visitors, something bad started to happen.

People needed to use the bathroom, but some of them did not use the toilets. Instead, they went behind the rocks. At first, this only happened sometimes, but soon it became a big problem. The park rangers, who protect the park, had to clean up after these people. They were very surprised and upset.

The rangers put portable toilets in the park, but still, some people did not use them. They went to the rocks instead and left a mess. This made the park dirty and unsafe.

The rangers were very frustrated, so they made a sign. The sign said, “PLEASE DO NOT POOP IN THE ROCKS. WE CAN’T BELIEVE WE HAD TO MAKE THIS SIGN.”

The sign was at the start of the trail where everyone could see it. People thought it was funny. They took pictures of the sign and shared them online. They laughed, but they also understood the message.

The park became famous because of the sign. People came to see it and the beautiful park. Over time, the sign worked. Fewer people made a mess, and the park was clean again. The rangers were happy.

Now, the sign is part of the park’s story. It is a funny but important reminder to respect nature.

### Don’t Poop In The Rocks: B2

In a remote and scenic national park nestled in the rugged mountains, a bizarre problem began to unfold, much to the dismay of the park rangers. The park, known for its beautiful rock formations and serene hiking trails, had become a popular destination for nature lovers and outdoor enthusiasts. However, with the increase in visitors, the rangers started to notice an alarming and unsanitary trend.

Hikers, apparently in desperate need, had taken to relieving themselves among the rocks scattered across the landscape. At first, it was just a rare occurrence, but as time went on, it became a significant issue. The rangers, shocked by the behavior, found themselves on patrols not just to protect wildlife, but also to clean up after the less considerate visitors.

Despite placing portable restrooms at key points along the trails, the problem persisted. Visitors seemed to ignore the facilities provided and instead sought out secluded spots among the rocks, leaving behind an unpleasant mess that not only marred the beauty of the park but also posed a health hazard.

Frustrated and bewildered, the rangers decided to take a drastic step. They created a sign that was blunt, straightforward, and left no room for misinterpretation: “PLEASE DO NOT POOP IN THE ROCKS. WE CAN’T BELIEVE WE HAD TO MAKE THIS SIGN.”

The sign, placed at the trailhead where all visitors would see it, quickly became a subject of curiosity and amusement. Hikers would stop to take pictures, share the sign on social media, and laugh at the absurdity of the situation. However, the sign also served its intended purpose—it made people think twice before deciding to treat the natural rock formations as a personal restroom.

As word of the sign spread, the park gained an odd sort of fame. Visitors came not just for the breathtaking views, but also to see the infamous sign in person. It became a symbol of the rangers’ tireless efforts to maintain the park’s beauty and cleanliness, and of the unexpected challenges that come with managing a popular natural destination.

Over time, the sign did its job. The number of unsightly incidents decreased, and the park slowly returned to its pristine state. The rangers, while still baffled by the need for such a sign, were relieved to see their beloved park being treated with the respect it deserved.

The story of the sign became a part of the park’s lore, a humorous yet poignant reminder of the importance of respecting nature and the sometimes ridiculous lengths required to preserve it.
