## Who they are
Adult Czech self-learners of English, most evidence pointing toward people
in their 40s–50s who have studied before and doubt their own ability —
**medium** (research, 2026-09-14). One data point suggests some arrive not
by actively shopping for a course but by first liking Lenka's own personality
and enthusiasm — **low**, watch for a pattern rather than treating as settled.

## Core problem, in their own words
Years of studying English but still can't understand native speakers or
speak with confidence — **medium** (Petr Titěra, Romana Svobodová, Dáša
Nastulczyková testimonials).

## Emotional drivers
- Fear of being incapable, often tied explicitly to age ("moc jsem
  nedoufala, že se něco naučím" at 48) — **medium**.
- Relief and surprise at a felt sense of fluency, framed as "not an
  unsolvable problem after all" — **low**.

## Past attempts that failed them
Generic or interchangeable courses that didn't crack listening comprehension
specifically, and didn't feel different from "what everyone knows" — **low**,
two independent quotes. This is the evidenced version of "zkoušel/a jsem už
spoustu kurzů a nefungovalo to" — real, but resting on old (2019–2021)
testimonials, not fresh evidence.

## What they refuse to do
No evidence yet. Open.

## The transformation they want, in their language
A daily habit that actually sticks ("neumím si představit den bez
angličtiny"), and a felt sense of moving forward rather than re-covering old
ground — **medium** for the habit theme, **low** for the "moving forward" framing.

## What they believe success hinges on
A structured plan that forgives imperfect completion — credit given to the
plan's design, not to their own discipline — **low**, one specific quote.

## Objections seen in the wild
- Age as a disqualifier — **medium**.
- Doubt that this course will be different from ones already tried —
  **low**.

## Interpretation (not fact — see the dossier for the full reasoning)
The strongest emotional pull in the evidence is not about content (grammar,
vocabulary) but about permission: being told it's fine to progress
imperfectly, and proof that age or past failure doesn't disqualify someone.
Unconfirmed — needs fresh, unprompted evidence before it should drive real
copy decisions.
