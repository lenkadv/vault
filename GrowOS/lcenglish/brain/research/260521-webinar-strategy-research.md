# Selling Online Courses via Webinars in 2025–2026: A Practical Field Guide for a Czech Online English Course

## TL;DR

- **Webinars still work — but only as evolved "workshops" with shorter run-times (35–60 min), an offer designed BEFORE the slides, and a follow-up funnel that does roughly half the heavy lifting on replay.** The 2019 "90-minute hype reel" format is dead; the 2025 format is "give a real mini-result, then sell."
- **For a Czech adult English course, the highest-leverage play in 2026 is NOT a one-off live webinar but a paid or free 5–7 day micro-challenge (e.g. "5 dní s angličtinou — rozmluvte se za týden") that ends in a 45-minute live masterclass + offer.** The Czech market is 3–5 years behind US trends and there is essentially no CZ English-course competitor using this format — first-mover advantage is real.
- **Skip the hype gurus. The grounded operators worth learning from in 2025–2026 are Jason Fladlien (offer-first webinar mechanics), Stu McLaren (membership + relaunches), Colin Boyd (Sell From Stage / non-sleazy delivery), and — for the CZ context — Dušan Souček and the Mioweb/FAPI ecosystem.** Amy Porterfield's flagship "Webinars That Convert" was sunsetted; treat that as a signal, not the death of the format.

## Key Findings

1. **The format is alive; the script changed.** Goldcast's 2025 B2B Webinar Benchmark Report (19,531 webinars across 418 brands) reports a **225% increase in webinars across B2B organizations since its previous report** and an average live attendance rate of 33% of registrants (up from 29%). Average viewing time per attendee sits at ~51 minutes.
2. **Length is bifurcating.** Per BigMarker's attendee-preference data (widely cited by Findstack, Zippia, Influno, etc.): "44% of webinar attendees prefer sessions to last 45 minutes. 41% said that they'd like them to be 30 minutes long. Only 10% said that they'd like them to go a full hour." Actual format reality: the most common bucket is still 45–60 minutes (58% of all webinars per Goldcast), but short-form (<30 min) tripled from 3% to 11% in one year.
3. **Conversion math has tightened.** Live webinars convert ~33% of attendees on average; on-demand converts ~25%. Industry-average overall "webinar conversion" sits around 56% (registration-to-attend) and 2–3% landing-page-to-buyer for the full funnel. Crucially, 40–44% of total webinar views now happen on replay (ClickMeeting) — your post-event sequence is co-equal with the live event.
4. **"Challenge" is the new webinar.** 5-day, 7-day and 14-day challenges (paid or free), ending in a closing masterclass, now consistently outperform pure webinars for course launches because they deliver a felt mini-transformation BEFORE the pitch. Paid challenges hit ~70–80% completion vs. <5% for traditional self-paced courses (CommuniPass 2026 cohort data — caveat: vendor-published).
5. **Amy Porterfield retired "Webinars That Convert"** — her flagship since the mid-2010s — and now teaches a lighter "Webinar Blueprint" inside her broader business curriculum. Coach Chris Michael Harris flagged this explicitly in 2025: "Take Amy Porterfield's Webinars That Convert — that was her flagship for over a decade. Sunsetted. Done." The format she taught (60–90 min, education-heavy, single CTA) is what got tired; the medium did not.
6. **Jason Fladlien remains the most credible technician.** His central 2025 advice (EmailMarketingHeroes podcast): design the OFFER first, the slides second; live still outperforms evergreen for him; the "stack close" still works; and webinar selling now lives under names like "masterclass," "workshop" and "challenge" but the mechanics are unchanged. Direct quote: "An email list and a webinar are my go-to in almost every market for the best way to make money."
7. **Stu McLaren has pivoted from launches to recurring revenue.** His 2024–2025 work centres on Membership.io and the "Founding Member Launch Accelerator" (Hamilton, ON, Nov 13–14, 2024) — short, cohort-style relaunches rather than annual one-shot webinar pushes.
8. **The Czech market is roughly 3–5 years behind.** Dušan Souček (dusansoucek.cz) is the most publicly-active CZ practitioner, openly stating in his April 2024 article "8 způsobů jak prodávat online kurzy v roce 2024": "V našich projektech nám webináře dělají téměř polovinu všech tržeb… Můj oblíbený je evergreenový mód, kdy vysíláme každý den." David Kirš (SmartEmailing/Mioweb founder, who imported the model from Brendon Burchard in ~2012) formally stopped selling his own programs in 2022. Tooling is dominated by Mioweb (2,000 Kč + DPH/month) + FAPI for payments + SmartEmailing for email.
9. **No Czech online English-course seller currently uses a real webinar/challenge funnel.** Broňa Sobotka (CZ's biggest English YouTube channel, ~188k subs in early 2025) sells through Seduo.cz; OnlineJazyky/EasyLingo runs a subscription + affiliate model (399 Kč/month; 8.69M Kč paid in affiliate commissions since 2017); Help for English uses a freemium app (Englishme, 189 Kč/month); James Cook Languages serves B2B corporates. **The webinar/challenge funnel niche for a CZ adult English course is essentially uncontested.**

## Details

### 1. Is the webinar still a relevant sales mechanism? (Yes — with caveats)

The "webinars are dead" claim is wrong, but the format has shifted significantly. Hard data from 2024–2025:

- **B2B usage is up dramatically, not down.** Goldcast's 2025 B2B Webinar Benchmark Report: "Since our last report, there's been a 225% increase in webinars across B2B organizations." Per multiple GoToWebinar-sourced stat compilations (Outgrow, OrangeOwl, Martal): "73% of B2B marketers and sales leaders consider webinars as the most effective method for generating high-quality leads."
- **Attendance and engagement are healthy.** ON24's 2025 Digital Engagement Benchmarks Report (announced via Business Wire, March 11 2025) reports a 7% increase in average attendance (now 51 minutes), an 18% rise in demo bookings, and a 21% increase in CTA engagement per attendee.
- **But cold-traffic webinars are punished.** Dušan Souček explicitly warns: "Webináře na cold traffic nefungují moc dobře. Většinou dobře prodáváme na webináři, když na něj směřujeme už trošku předehřáté publikum." This matches the US consensus: webinars convert best on warm/lukewarm lists (email subscribers, retargeted social audiences, podcast listeners), not on cold Facebook/Meta ads to strangers.

**How webinars compare to alternative formats today:**

| Format | Best for | Conversion math | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Classic 60–75 min live webinar** | Warm list, ≤$1,000 offer, established brand | 5–15% of attendees | Medium; one shot |
| **Short masterclass / workshop (35–45 min)** | New, attention-saturated audiences | Similar conversion, ~30% higher show-up | Lower stress; replays better |
| **5-day / 7-day challenge** | Skeptical or under-confident audience (language learners FIT THIS PERFECTLY) | 10–15%+ if structured with daily wins | High — daily content for a week |
| **VSL (video sales letter)** | Pure paid-traffic, lower-ticket evergreen | 1–3% of viewers | Lowest ongoing; depends on copy |
| **Email-only launch** | Existing engaged list, price increase, retire-old-product | 1–5% of list | Low; not for new audiences |
| **Evergreen webinar funnel** | Scaling a proven live webinar | Usually ~50% of live conversion | High upfront, automated after |

For a Czech English course aimed at adults — i.e. an audience with **low confidence, high skepticism (they've often "tried English" before), and a need to feel a quick win** — a challenge or a short masterclass dramatically outperforms a static VSL.

### 2. Optimal webinar length and structure (2025–2026 consensus)

**Length:**
- **Sweet spot: 45–60 minutes total**, of which **30–40 min is teaching/experience and 15–20 min is the offer + Q&A**.
- BigMarker's attendee survey: 44% prefer 45 min, 41% prefer 30 min, only 10% want 60+ min.
- 90-minute webinars now see significant drop-off after ~48 minutes (ClickMeeting drop-off heatmaps).
- BUT: do not crash the close. Jason Fladlien's mechanic — "the offer is the punchline; the setup has to be earned" — still demands 25–35 min of real value before pitching, or trust collapses.

**Structure that works in 2025 (synthesized from Fladlien, Boyd, ClickMeeting data, Goldcast):**

1. **0–5 min: Hook + housekeeping.** Promise a specific, named outcome ("by the end of this 45 minutes you'll know exactly which 3 grammar mistakes are blocking you from speaking — and have fixed one of them live"). Promise a stay-to-the-end bonus.
2. **5–10 min: Your origin / authority story.** Why this method, why you, why now. Keep it under 5 minutes — Colin Boyd's research shows long origin stories tank engagement.
3. **10–30 min: The "mini-transformation" core teaching.** ClickMeeting's analysis of high-converting webinars is unambiguous: conversion is decided 15–20 minutes BEFORE the CTA slide, when the attendee thinks "this could actually work for me." For a language course this means one tangible, named technique demonstrated live, ideally with a participant volunteer.
4. **30–35 min: Bridge / transition.** Permission-based ("can I show you how we work with people who want to go deeper?"). This is the moment Russell Brunson's "Perfect Webinar" handles via the "permission to pitch" line; works regardless of niche.
5. **35–50 min: Offer + stack + scarcity.** Stack the value (course + community + live calls + bonus), price reveal, real deadline (a 48–72 hour bonus window, not a fake countdown).
6. **50–60 min: Live Q&A.** Many sales close here, not during the pitch. **Do not** cut Q&A short to hit the hour mark.

**Education-to-pitch ratio:** Roughly **70/30** is the modern consensus (down from the old 80/20). With shorter formats, the pitch density has to be higher per minute, but not by deception — by clarity of offer.

### 3. Key shifts in the last three years (2022–2025)

1. **Show-up rates softened, then partially recovered.** Goldcast's 2024 benchmark put the typical live show-up at "a little less than 30%" of registrants — down from 40–50% pre-2022. Their 2025 report puts it at 33%, "up since our last report, when about 29% of people who registered also attended." Build your funnel math around 25–33%, not 50%.
2. **On-demand has nearly equalled live.** ON24's 2025 report: "45% of attendees opted to watch webinars on-demand, reinforcing the need for flexible content consumption." ClickMeeting puts replay at 40–44% of total views. Your replay sequence (emails + replay-page) is no longer optional.
3. **The "challenge" format has matured.** Zach Spuckler ("Heart, Soul & Hustle") and Pat Flynn-adjacent operators have moved most of their launches from pure webinars to 5-day challenges, citing show-up resilience: "Webinars are unforgiving — you've got 90 minutes to make the sale and do everything right. With a challenge you've got five days; if Day 1 doesn't land, you've got Day 2, 3, 4, 5."
4. **The "workshop" / "masterclass" rebrand is real but partial.** Audiences are fatigued by some webinar-marketing tropes — yet Goldcast found that events EXPLICITLY titled "webinar" actually outperformed alternatively-named events by 66% on registrations in 2024, because audiences now associate "webinar" with substantive content again. **Tactical takeaway:** use the word "masterclass" or "workshop" in copy, but don't be embarrassed to also say "webinar" — it's no longer a dirty word.
5. **AI repurposing changed the economics.** Goldcast users repurposed 19,000+ webinars into clips/blogs/social in 2024 — saving an estimated $650,000 in labor. A single 45-minute webinar should now produce 8–12 short-form videos, 2–3 blog posts, and a long-form email sequence as a matter of course.
6. **Mechanism-driven offers replaced "transformation" offers.** Per Chris Michael Harris's 2025 essay: "Courses as we knew them are on life support unless they're hyper-specific, mechanism-driven transformations. No more vague 'become your best self' vibes." For language learning this means: not "learn English," but "the 3-step Czech-to-English thought-bridge method that fixes the speaking freeze."
7. **Cohort + community has displaced pure self-study.** CommuniPass 2026 data: <5% completion on static courses vs. 70–80% on paid challenges. Refunds and chargebacks track completion. The course-only model is structurally weakening.

### 4. The English-speaking market leaders worth following (the grounded ones)

Author of *One to Many* (still the single best book on the format). 2025 advice on the EmailMarketingHeroes podcast: **start with the offer, not the slides**; live > evergreen for most operators; "an email list and a webinar are my go-to in almost every market." **For technique, he is the gold standard.**

**Colin Boyd** (Sell From Stage Academy) — Amy Porterfield's go-to expert on the actual delivery and "infusion selling" mechanics. Episode #554 of *The Amy Porterfield Show* (March 2023): "Too much information is one of the biggest mistakes in webinars. Infusion selling is speaking your content in a way that sells your offer." Best resource for *how to actually pitch without feeling sleazy*.

**Stu McLaren** — Pivoted from one-shot launches to memberships and "Founding Member Launches" via Membership.io. His 2024 Founding Member Launch Accelerator (Hamilton, ON, Nov 13–14, 2024) signals the direction: 1–2 day intensive cohorts, recurring revenue, less reliance on annual mega-launches. Best for anyone who wants to layer a paid community on top of a course (highly relevant for a language course).

**Amy Porterfield** — Still influential as a podcaster and "first-business" coach; **but has sunset Webinars That Convert**. Now teaches a lighter "Webinar Blueprint" inside her broader curriculum. Worth following for the principles, not the latest tactics.

**Zach Spuckler / Heart, Soul & Hustle** — Smaller operator, but the most credible voice on 5-day challenge launches. Quote: "Webinars are very unforgiving… with five days, if Day 1 doesn't work amazing, you've got Day 2, 3, 4, 5."

**Russell Brunson** — His "Perfect Webinar" script ($7) is dated (2014-era) but the structural bones (hook → story → 3 secrets → stack → close) still work. Useful as a skeleton, not a verbatim playbook.

**Honorable mentions:** Alessio Pieroni (Scale For Impact; ex-Mindvalley CMO, 114+ webinar funnels claimed responsible for $100M+ in sales for clients including Tony Robbins and Marisa Peer); Pat Flynn (Smart Passive Income) for evergreen and email; Destini Copp for challenge mechanics in small businesses.

**Specifically NOT recommended** for grounded SMB operators: anyone whose marketing centers on private jets, supercars, or "first $1M in 30 days." The serious operators above are conspicuously low-key.

### 5. The Czech / Slovak market: 3–5 years behind, and that's an opportunity

**Where the CZ scene actually is today (2024–2026):**

- **Most active publicly-teaching practitioner:** **Dušan Souček** (dusansoucek.cz). Openly states "v našich projektech nám webináře dělají téměř polovinu všech tržeb" and runs an EverWebinar evergreen funnel that broadcasts daily. His 2024 article "8 způsobů jak prodávat online kurzy v roce 2024" is the best plain-Czech roadmap.
- **Yasmin Žitníková** (jasnastrategie.cz) sells "Mega webinář," a course teaching the free-webinar → paid-course model; testimonials cite 3,000–6,000 EUR per single webinar in the CZ/SK market.
- **David Kirš** — the architect of the Czech infopodnikání wave from 2012 onwards — formally stopped selling his own programs in 2022 ("Ukončil jsem zatím prodej svých online vzdělávacích programů… vracím se do Miowebu"). Still owns SmartSelling a.s. (parent of Mioweb, FAPI, SmartEmailing), but is no longer the active teacher he once was.
- **Daniel Gamrot** — Productivity/AI specialist; sells individual webinar recordings (e.g. "Vibe Coding," 490 Kč; "Pod kapotou" live event, May 2026). Single-purchase model, no funnel.
- **Filip Dřímalka** (drimalka.com / Aibility / Digiskills) — pivoted entirely to AI/digital-transformation lectures for corporates; B2B consulting model, not classic infopodnikání.
- **Tooling dominance:** Mioweb (2,000 Kč + DPH/month, the "česká jednička pro online kurzy" since 2012) + FAPI (payments/membership) + SmartEmailing (email) is the de facto stack. SimpleShop (Redbit, 17,489 sellers claimed) is the main alternative. For webinars, serious players use translated US tools — WebinarJam and EverWebinar — there is no native Czech webinar SaaS of comparable maturity.
- **Format reality:** the canonical CZ method is still the 60–90 minute live webinar + 7–14 day cart-open campaign. The 5-day challenge model is essentially absent from the CZ infopodnikání mainstream. "Workshop"/"masterclass" rebranding has barely begun.

**What this means strategically:** **the Czech market is roughly where the US market was in 2019–2020**. The good news: most of the modern English-language tactics (shorter masterclass, challenge format, mechanism-driven offer, AI repurposing) will feel novel and over-deliver in CZ.

### 6. Specific recommendations for an online English course for Czech adults

This is the most defensible angle in the whole report. The competitive landscape:

- **Broňa Sobotka (brona.cz)** — biggest CZ English-learning YouTube channel (~188k subs as of early 2025). Sells through **Seduo.cz** (subscription videocourses) and bundles on his own site. **Does not run live webinars or challenges as a sales mechanism.** His only "webinar" appearance was as a guest on Digiskills' "Digitální Safari #27" (Dec 2024).
- **OnlineJazyky.cz / EasyLingo** — subscription SaaS (399 Kč/month), affiliate-driven (paid 8.69M Kč in affiliate commissions since 2017). 434,000+ students claimed. No webinar funnel.
- **Help for English (Marek Vít)** — free portal + Englishme app (189 Kč/month). Runs occasional free webinars **for teachers** (e.g., "Englishme pro učitele," 23 Jan 2025, zdarma), not for end-learners.
- **James Cook Languages (jcl.cz)** — B2B corporate training for 600+ companies, MS Teams classes. Not consumer-facing infopodnikání.
- **Hana Stejskalová, conversational tutors ("english with bara"), Konverzační klub** — traditional 1-on-1 / small-group bookings or subscription. No webinar/challenge funnels.

**Conclusion:** The webinar/challenge-funnel niche for an English course targeting Czech adults is **essentially uncontested**. This is rare.

**Why a challenge in particular fits language learning:**

1. **The single biggest pain point of CZ adult English learners is the "speaking freeze" — they read/understand at B1+ but won't open their mouth.** Susanne Rieker's 5-day challenge case study ("5 Days to Plan Your First Online Course") is a near-perfect template structure: in 5 days the learner can't master English, but they CAN have their first three real-life conversations and break a psychological block. That felt mini-result IS the proof you need to convert into the paid course.
2. **Daily action structure mirrors how a language is actually learned.** A 5-day micro-challenge ("5 dní mluvení anglicky — i když si myslíte, že to nezvládnete") is congruent with the product you're selling. A 90-minute webinar is not.
3. **The CZ market has been conditioned by Duolingo's streak mechanic.** A "5-day challenge" piggybacks on a behavioral pattern Czech adults already know.
4. **AI tutors are commoditizing self-study.** Your moat is **community + accountability + a real human voice in Czech explaining specifically how a Czech speaker should think in English.** A challenge format gives all three; a static course gives none.

**Recommended specific structure for the CZ English course:**

- **Lead magnet:** A 10–15 minute "Diagnostický test, proč se nemůžete rozmluvit anglicky" + free PDF "3 chyby, které dělá 90 % Čechů, kteří se učí anglicky 10 let."
- **Pre-launch warm-up (2–3 weeks):** Daily short videos on YouTube/Reels + email nurture.
- **Free 5-day challenge ("5 dní s angličtinou — rozmluvte se za týden"):** Daily 15–20 min video + 10-minute task delivered via email + a private Telegram or Facebook group. Day 5 culminates in a 45–60 min live masterclass + offer.
- **Live masterclass structure:** 5 min hook → 5 min story → 30 min teaching ("Bridge Method" or whatever you name your mechanism) → 5 min transition → 15 min offer + stack → 15 min Q&A. **Total: 75 min** (slightly above 60 because Q&A is where CZ buyers decide; cutting it would be a mistake in this market).
- **Offer:** Don't sell a course alone. Sell **course + 8-week cohort + weekly live conversation circle + AI tutor + community**. Anchor at 4,990–7,990 Kč with a 48-hour fast-action bonus.
- **Tools:** Mioweb (membership + landing pages) + FAPI (payments) + SmartEmailing (or Ecomail) + EverWebinar/WebinarJam OR ClickMeeting for live + a free Discord/Telegram for the challenge group.
- **Follow-up:** A 14-day evergreen email sequence to non-buyers, then enrolment closed for 60–90 days to preserve scarcity.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (next 30 days) — Validate the offer before building anything:**

1. **Write the sales page first.** Before recording lessons, write the long-form sales page for the cohort offer. If it doesn't compel you to buy, the course won't compel anyone else.
2. **Run a 60-minute live workshop on Zoom to 20–50 existing contacts** (email list, LinkedIn, Facebook friends). Pitch the cohort. If 5–10% buy at full price, the offer is real. If not, fix the offer, not the funnel.
3. **Identify your "mechanism."** Name it. Czech learners respond to specific named methods ("Metoda mostu," "3-krokový systém," etc.) far better than generic "speak English better."

**Stage 2 (days 30–90) — Build the challenge funnel:**

4. **Run a free 5-day challenge to a list of 200–500 people.** Aim for 10–20 paid cohort spots. Even one round gives you the data to optimize.
5. **Record every challenge day; build the evergreen version on round 3 or later.** Do not go evergreen until you have a live-converting version. Souček's own warning applies: "Pozor, že na cold traffic webináře nefungují moc dobře" — and that goes tenfold for evergreen funnels.
6. **Set up the replay funnel from day one.** 40–45% of your views will come from replays. Email 1 (replay link, send within 2 hours of webinar end), Email 2 (objection-handling, +24h), Email 3 (case study, +48h), Email 4 (deadline reminder, +60h), Email 5 (last call, +71h).

**Stage 3 (days 90–180) — Scale what's proven:**

7. **Add paid traffic only after a live launch has converted at ≥3% list-to-buyer.** Below that threshold, ads make losses bigger, not smaller.
8. **Layer in a low-ticket front-end (1,490–1,990 Kč) "starter course"** to recover ad spend and prime cold traffic before the main cohort offer. This is the modern Brunson/Hormozi "trust bridge" play.
9. **Re-run the cohort every 60–90 days, not monthly.** Quarterly cadence keeps scarcity real and gives you time to ship a quality experience between cohorts.

**Thresholds that would change these recommendations:**

- **If your live cohort show-up rate drops below 20% of registrants**, your audience is too cold. Pull back, build the email list first via free content, do not run another launch.
- **If your masterclass converts >15% of attendees**, you have a winner — start scaling ad spend immediately and build the evergreen version.
- **If completion of your paid challenge falls below 50%**, the challenge is too hard or the daily commitment too high. Cut the daily ask to 10 minutes max.
- **If refunds exceed 8%**, your offer over-promises relative to the experience. Fix the onboarding or downgrade the headline promise — do not just tighten the refund policy.

## Caveats

- **The aggregated webinar conversion statistics (~56% conversion, etc.) are dominated by B2B SaaS data from large platforms (ON24, Goldcast, Zoom, Univid).** For a B2C language course sold to Czech individuals, expect lower live attendance (20–30%) and lower live-conversion percentages (3–8% of attendees buying), but compensating volume from replays and cold-but-warmed-up retargeting audiences.
- **Several of the cited "best practice" sources are vendor-published (Goldcast, ClickMeeting, ON24, EasyWebinar, Hubilo, BigMarker).** They have a structural incentive to portray webinars as healthier than they are. The countervailing voices (Chris Michael Harris, Susanne Rieker, CommuniPass) explicitly flag webinar-funnel decay and are worth weighting accordingly. Note also that ON24's own marketing has cited "50%" for on-demand viewing while its 2025 press release states 45%; vendor numbers should be cross-checked.
- **The "courses are dying" framing in the CommuniPass 2026 content is itself speculative** — it is a platform marketing its own pivot toward interactive challenges. The data point (5% static-course completion) is widely cited but the implication that all static courses are "on life support" is editorial.
- **The Czech market analysis is based on publicly visible signals (websites, podcasts, public testimonials) as of late 2025–early 2026.** There are almost certainly private operators running profitable challenge funnels in CZ who simply don't blog about it; my conclusion that "the niche is uncontested" applies to the *publicly visible* competitive landscape.
- **Amy Porterfield sunsetting "Webinars That Convert" is documented but the exact date is unclear from public sources.** It is cited via Chris Michael Harris's 2025 essay rather than a direct Porterfield announcement.
- **Jason Fladlien's quarter-billion-dollar webinar revenue claim is self-reported** and includes affiliate launches he hosted but did not own. The mechanics he teaches are nevertheless industry-consensus.
- **Czech labour-market and consumer-spending data for online English courses in 2025–2026 were not part of this research scope.** Pricing recommendations (4,990–7,990 Kč) are based on competitive observation (Mioweb pricing, EasyLingo subscriptions, typical CZ infopodnikání price points) not on a primary affordability study.
