## Who we are really up against
- **English Hacker** (Markéta Růžičková, englishhacker.cz) — solo-founder online
  course, built on her own learning story, large free blog — **unchecked**
- **Broňa.cz** (Bronislav Sobotka) — by far the biggest name here: 188.000
  YouTube subscribers, regular Czech media coverage, course sold on
  marketplaces (Seduo.cz: 98 ratings, 80 five-star) — **unchecked**. Lenka's
  own read (personal, 2026-09-14, she knows him and does not like him — noted
  as context, not evidence): scale may put him in a different league rather
  than direct competition; his content reads to her as describing grammar and
  vocabulary items rather than teaching an actual method — he may have found
  what makes people *feel* they are learning, without it being a real system.
  Unverified opinion, not checked against his actual course content.
- **Help for English** (helpforenglish.cz) — large free content hub +
  partner network (Italki, Online Jazyky, englishme!); now also sells a paid
  app at 189 Kč/měsíc — **unchecked**. Lenka does not know what they actually
  sell beyond that — open question, not researched further this pass.
- **Dream English** (dreamenglish.cz) — "51 days" promise; site gives almost
  nothing away publicly on purpose — **unchecked**. Lenka's own read: sales run
  hard through evergreen webinars (not visible on the public site, which is
  why it looked so thin), method is drill-based per what Dream English itself
  says. This is a live example of an evergreen-webinar funnel actually running
  in this exact market — relevant context for LCEnglish's own funnel decisions.
- **KubovaEnglish** (kubovaenglish.cz, "Kuba") — solo creator, individual
  topic courses ~2.000–3.000 Kč, now also selling an AI tutor product per
  Lenka. Per Lenka's own read: tone is much more irreverent/crude, likely a
  different, younger audience; his pull is his social-media presence and how
  outspoken he is, not (in her view) course quality — **unchecked, personal
  opinion**.
- **A second, even more crude/outspoken "lektor"** — Lenka named this as a
  real competitor but could not recall the name in this session. Candidate,
  not yet identified — follow up when the name surfaces.
- **Duolingo / apps** — free, gamified, "something is better than nothing" —
  the real alternative for a lot of this audience is not a paid course, it's
  an app or doing nothing — **unchecked**

## What they promise (their own claims)
- English Hacker: fun, effective, made from her own story of learning to speak.
- Broňa: fear of mistakes — not English itself — is the real obstacle.
- Help for English: everything you need, free, on one site.
- Dream English: "a new way to learn English and never forget it again."
- KubovaEnglish: step-by-step, 30 days, "the most famous Czech English teacher."

## Where they are strong (evidence, dated 2026-09-14)
- English Hacker: large free content library, personal founder story,
  student praise for how simply/warmly lessons are explained.
- Broňa: scale nobody else on this list is close to (188k YouTube, national
  media coverage, dyslexia/dysgraphia personal story that broadens his appeal).
- Help for English: sheer size and longevity of free content, a real partner
  ecosystem, now monetizing via a low-price app too.
- Dream English / KubovaEnglish: not enough evidence gathered yet to say.

## Where they leave people down (evidence, dated 2026-09-14)
- English Hacker: per a third-party review roundup, no mobile app, no
  offline mode, materials are static PDF/Word/MP3 downloads — nothing
  interactive online.
- Broňa: no weakness found this pass — say so honestly rather than inventing one.
- Help for English: not reassessed this pass.
- Dream English / KubovaEnglish: not enough evidence gathered yet to say.

## What everyone in this market says
- "Konečně se rozmluvíte." / "Přestanete se bát mluvit."
- A number of days or minutes a day is always the hook ("15 minut denně",
  "za 51 dní", "za 30 dní", "za 14 dní" — LCEnglish's own Nepravidelná slovesa
  uses the same shape of promise).
- Fear of speaking, not grammar knowledge, is the named enemy almost everywhere.

## The angle that is ours
The one evidence-based difference this pass actually supports: LCEnglish's
Shadowloop app gives interactive speaking practice across all three courses,
where English Hacker's own reviewers name static downloadable materials as a
real gap. That is checked against English Hacker specifically.

**Corrected 2026-09-14, same pass:** the first version of this file said no
evidence-based difference was found against Broňa or Help for English. That
set the bar too high — a real difference does not require a competitor's own
customer to have complained about the gap; checking what they actually sell
is enough.

- **Against Broňa:** everything read this pass (his own site, his Seduo
  listing, press interviews) describes his course as 290 video lessons — no
  mention anywhere of an interactive practice app. Shadowloop's cross-course
  speaking practice is a real, checked difference against Broňa specifically.
- **Against Help for English:** their new paid app, EnglishMe! (189 Kč/month),
  is a vocabulary and listening tool — tens of thousands of example sentences,
  18.000+ words with audio, structured "learning plans" (per third-party
  descriptions of the app, not a hands-on check of it). Nothing found
  describes shadowing-style speaking repetition. The difference likely still
  holds, but more softly than against Broňa, since this reads secondhand
  rather than from using the app directly.

Shadowing / Shadowloop as a cross-course differentiator is Lenka's own read
(confirmed in conversation, 2026-09-14). This pass upgrades it from "untested
opinion" to "checked against what these two competitors actually sell,
evidence found in favor" — not yet "verified by using their products directly."
