Random acts of humor — just like random acts of marketing — do nothing for your bottom line. But jokes that sell are honestly among the most powerful things in modern marketing. Jokes that sell immediately show your audience that you get it — you understand them, their world, and the problems they're facing. And that's important, because you're offering to solve those problems, so they need to believe in you.

But the benefits don't stop there.

**The power of jokes that sell:**

**One: longer watch time.** If your content's entertaining, it's going to keep your audience engaged — which means they watch for longer, stay on page for longer, and that means higher consumption of the sales message. Higher consumption equals more sales.

**Two: you can embed the sales message inside jokes.** Jokes are a great place to embed a hidden truth. People only laugh at a joke because it's true — not because you're funny. So if they laugh at a joke about how bad their problem is, they're basically agreeing it needs solving. You're embedding the sales message into the humor. By the same virtue, you can make them laugh at how useless all the other solutions are — invalidating your competition with comedy.

**Three: you can paint the future.** If the future state you're painting raises a smile or a chuckle, it feels true and real — and you reinforce the idea that they want that future.

This is why you need an entertaining mini VSL on your sales pages, in your ads, and anywhere you can slot it into your marketing machine.

You won't be surprised to hear me — the creative director of White Label Comedy — tell you that comedy is hands down the most powerful tool in your marketing toolkit. But I'm not the only one saying it. David Ogilvy, the godfather of modern advertising, said: "The best ideas come as jokes. Make your thinking as funny as possible." David Sugarman, who built a nine-figure direct response empire, says humor is one of the most effective ways to sell. And Robert Collier — the OG of persuasion — puts it plainly: "Make your reader smile, you've built a bridge of connection. Make them laugh, and you've paved a path to conversion."

### Self-shooting on a budget

**Adam:** You've got your script. Now let's talk production.

Not everyone has a TV studio and expensive cameras — and the good news is you don't need them.

**Camera:** iPhone. And I always use the front-facing camera, not the back — because I use an autocue app on the same screen. PromptSmart Pro (no affiliation) scrolls automatically based on your voice, so it always has the next bit ready. You don't need to learn your lines or manually adjust the scroll speed. Just sit the iPhone on a $10–15 tripod and you're good.

**Background:** Either go all out (shelves, books, soft LED lighting) or keep it completely simple. Don't go halfway — either commit or keep it simple.

**Lighting:** Natural light from a window is your best friend. If you're shooting at night or in a dark room, a ring light or a desk lamp bounced off a white wall makes a big difference. Natural light first.

**Audio:** This is the one thing viewers won't forgive. You can have a badly shot video — you can shoot while walking down the street — and it'll kind of work. But bad, clippy, crackly audio? People will stop watching. Invest in a lapel mic or USB mic. Even $30 beats built-in camera audio. Record in a quiet room with soft furnishings. If it's still echoey: bring a duvet into the room — it absorbs more than you'd expect.

**Overall tip:** Don't overthink it. Speed to market matters more than a perfect setup. If the messaging is right and the jokes are there, nobody cares whether you've got a sexy backdrop. Don't buy expensive kit just to shoot your first VSL.

### AI tools for faceless offers

**Adam:** I'm not blindly pro-AI. I wouldn't use AI to write the script — use your human brain for that. But for other parts:

**Slide deck:** Gamma.app. Drop the script in, it builds a version of the slides that you can then edit. Dramatically speeds up time to publish.

**AI avatars:** Synthesia, D-ID, HeyGen. HeyGen is currently the best for delivering jokes with human-like timing — and timing is everything with humor. Poor delivery can be deadly. If you're going faceless, test more than one tool. Be critical. Go back over sections, give the avatar notes, make sure the jokes actually land. Mix avatar with text-on-screen moments and B-roll every 10–15 seconds — pure avatar video feels monotonous.

### Additional visuals

**Rule:** Script first, visuals later. Never make visuals before you've approved the cut — you may want to change the script, and then those visuals are wasted.

*(Adam: I once spent two days animating a machine that took brains on a conveyor belt and pumped out laughs — a metaphor for our writing team. Then I watched the video and thought "this doesn't make sense here, I want to change that line." Two days gone. Script first.)*

**Three types of visuals:**

**1. B-roll footage:** Stock video (Pexels, Pixabay — free; or AI-generated via Adobe Firefly, Freepik). Visualize what you're talking about — don't be too clever. When you mention a problem, show the problem. When you describe a solution, show the solution.

**2. Text overlays:** Emphasize key points with animated text. What would be bold or a headline on a sales page — put it on screen. Helps viewers watching without sound.

**3. Comedy creatives** (if you have a subscription): Browse the frameworks library — use them at key points in the VSL, just as you'd use them in image ads. They all either amplify a pain or amplify a desire or solution.
