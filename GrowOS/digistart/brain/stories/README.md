# Stories

> This folder holds your real stories: the moments, lessons, and turning points
> from your business and life that make your marketing human. The system reaches
> for these to make a post or email land. One file per story. This README explains
> the folder; you do not fill it in.

## Why stories matter

Facts tell, stories sell. A real moment from your world does more than any clever
line. This is where those moments live so the system can use them instead of
inventing something that never happened.

## The rules

- **Real only.** Every story here actually happened to you or your business. The
  system never makes up a story or changes what happened.
- **Keep it in your words.** Write the story the way you would tell it out loud.
  The system keeps your version true and does not twist the facts.
- **Date it.** The `when` field matters, so a story is never used out of its time.

## How to add a story

Copy `_template.md`, give it a short name (like `first-big-client.md`), and fill
it in: a title, when it happened, the story itself, and what it is good for
(the kind of point it helps you make).

## Picking one, and not wearing it out

A story lands the first time and grates the fourth. Your audience has a longer
memory than you think, and the fastest way to sound like a person running out of
material is to tell the same anecdote every month.

So the system picks by fit first — which story actually makes the point the piece
is making — and then, among stories that fit, reaches for one it has not used
lately. A story is not off limits after use; it just goes to the back of the
queue.

Two things you can set, both **inside the fields at the top of the file** — between
the two `---` lines, along with `when` and `use-for`. Not above them: a line before the
first `---` stops the fields being read at all, including by GrowOS.

- `status: retired` — the system stops reaching for it and never asks again. Add a
  line in the body saying why, so future-you knows.
- `use-only: launches` (or `newsletter`, or whatever fits) — kept for the occasions
  you name. Some stories are too good to spend on a Tuesday.

There is no field recording when a story was last used, so "not used lately" is a
judgement the system makes from what it can see in your recent work, not a counter
it keeps. If a story matters enough to ration exactly, `use-only` is the reliable
way to say so.

## Where a story may go

- **Your own stories** can go anywhere your voice goes.
- **A story about a customer needs their permission before it is published — and
  that includes with their name taken out.** A distinctive result, a place, a job
  title, or a sequence of events identifies someone perfectly well to anyone who
  knows them, and the person most likely to read it is the person it is about.
  Note in the file whether you have permission. Without a clear yes, the story
  stays internal: it can inform your thinking, and it does not go out in any form.
  If you believe a story is genuinely unidentifiable, that is your call to make
  explicitly in the file, not one the system makes for you.
- **A story with someone else's private details** — a client's numbers, a
  supplier's mistake — stays internal unless you say otherwise.

When no story fits, the system writes without one. A forced story is worse than
no story.
