## Přepis

Do you feel like you're straddling two AI worlds at the same time? I do. These days, my typical morning is full of deep work with AI: working on strategy and implementation, and vibe-coding agentic systems to power the future of my business. Then, in the afternoon, I'll get on a Q&A call with our community. They want to know whether it's really worth switching from ChatGPT to Claude, and whether it's actually important to have a paid account. Two different worlds, all in one day. And these days, which of those worlds you live in isn't decided by your current skill or technical sophistication; it's really just a choice, and that choice will shape what the next year looks like for your business.

In case we haven't met, hi. I'm Danny Eny. I run a business education company called Mirasi, and I recorded this as an update for the graduates of my AI strategist training and readers of my AI Curious book. But I'm opening it up to everyone, because even if you haven't trained with me yet, it's the clearest picture I can give you of where things stand. And this duality is exactly what the futurist William Gibson meant when he said, "The future is already here; it's just not evenly distributed." So let's talk about what's changed about the AI landscape that is important to every single person who uses it, and especially the people who are using it well, the way I trained you in AI strategist.

For most of the last year, I've been keeping a dynamically updating context document that I would feed into every conversation, basically a manual for my AI on who I am and how to work with me. Because skilled AI use is about leveraging the best parts of what the technology can do, and building the scaffolding for the stuff that it isn't good at yet. It's over 40,000 words long; that's the length of a book. It was a bit of a hassle, but it was worth it. As my students know, a big part of the power of AI is the context you give it. And while Claude already had cross-conversation memory, I needed more than it was able to give. And that context document is a big part of why my Claude was so much smarter than most people's, because my Claude had all that context. But fast forward to the summer of 2026, and all of a sudden Claude's memory capabilities took a giant leap forward, to the point where large parts of the document that I'd spent a year curating became redundant. Isn't that incredible? A year of hard work to build careful scaffolding that gave me a meaningful competitive advantage, being absorbed into a single Claude feature update.

That's the clock of innovation, as seen from my own desk. That clock moves at a crazy pace. As recently as 2022, AI couldn't reliably do basic arithmetic. By 2023 it could pass the bar exam. By 2024 it was writing production software. And this year, the models took another leap that made everything before them feel like a different era. But for most people, none of that matters. Because the clock that matters to them is the clock of adoption, and this one moves at human speed. It's constrained by trust, comfort, workflow integration, regulation, and habit change. And all of those things move slowly. I mean, email automation has been around for decades, and there are still plenty of entrepreneurs who aren't using it. That's why the Claude memory update didn't matter to most people, because they still aren't using AI at a level that made full use of even the earlier, more modest memory capabilities. Most people are still stuck on the most basic questions: which tool should I use? Can I trust what it tells me? Do I need the paid plan?

So what does this tell us about where the opportunity is today? This might surprise you; it surprises a lot of people. But the opportunity for most folks isn't on the frontier. Not because you can't do cool stuff there; of course you can, and it can be really powerful while it lasts. But it won't last long, because today's cutting edge is tomorrow's big yawn, like my 40,000-word context document. So play with the frontier if that's what gets you excited. But the real opportunity is in the gap between the two clocks, between what's already possible and what your customers and competitors are doing.

Here's a good for-example. Five years ago, Rough was the standard. You'd have a decent lead magnet, an okay sales page, and a video shot on a webcam in your kitchen. And that was good enough to look like you're in business. Today, that won't fly, because with AI, anyone can create polished versions of all that stuff, and you don't need much expertise or investment to make it happen. And sure, the stuff AI will produce by default is generic and mediocre, but still polished. So the floor has risen. The half-baked version that was good enough five years ago won't cut it today. But that shouldn't be discouraging, because AI is everywhere, and we've all seen the patterns, including your clients, who know what to look for. And if you're actually good at what you do, that's great news for you. It used to be that style would beat substance. But now that AI is making style accessible to everyone, it stops being meaningful as a differentiator. People know what to look for, and what they're looking for is substance. But that doesn't mean AI isn't important. It's a bad time to be mediocre, but it's also a bad time to be a Luddite. The real winning combination is deep subject matter expertise combined with skilled usage of AI.

And that's true even if AI never touches your actual work. Every week I hear from someone saying, "My work is human, so none of this affects me." And I understand where they're coming from. AI is technology, so I get why they think that if the technology can't do your job, you don't need to worry about any of this. But that's a dangerous mistake, because it isn't just about what AI can do; it's also about how AI is reshaping the landscape in which we all operate. Take a massage therapist, for example. AI can't give you a massage, and I don't think that'll change anytime soon. But that doesn't mean the therapist can't be augmented by AI. Like, maybe she records a quick voice note after each session about how it went. And then, before the next session, her AI reminds her that, "You've got Bob coming in today. Remember that his right shoulder has been an issue for three of the last five sessions. And ask about his daughter's internship." This is invisible to Bob, but it still affects him. His sense of it all is that, "Wow, she just really gets me." So he stays loyal. He tells his friends. The massage didn't change, but the experience did, and it gets harder and harder for the therapist down the street to compete. And obviously, this is just a single, narrow example, but there are a million more. Nobody and nothing are safe from AI disruption.

But here too, that doesn't have to be bad news. Because if you're taking this seriously, the innovation and adoption clocks are both working in your favor. The pace of innovation means that more and more capabilities are coming online, and it's easier and easier to take advantage of them. Think about the deep context that powers AI. Until recently, you needed clunky workarounds to take advantage of it. Now it's built right into the tools. And the pace of mainstream adoption means the gap between what is possible and what people are doing, or even what they know about, keeps getting wider. That gap is your opportunity.

So now let's get into the major recent developments that you need to be paying attention to. There are four of them, and I'll tell you for each one what it is, why it matters, and what you should be doing about it right now. The first one is what I've been telling you about, the memory capabilities of the frontier tools like Claude and ChatGPT. They've really come a long way. Now your AI can remember who you are, how you work, and what you're working on, across every conversation, without you doing anything clever. And if you know my work, then you know what a difference that makes, because everything else you do with AI gets better or worse depending on the context it has to work with. But to take advantage of that capability, there are two things you have to do. First, go into the settings and make sure all the memory capabilities are turned on. That's the annoying thing about these updates. Sometimes they roll out in turned-off mode, and people can't figure out why their AI doesn't seem to remember things. So make sure the features are turned on. And then, train your AI with all the context that it needs to serve you well. For example, using the protocols I give you in our AI strategist training, which I update and improve every time we run it.

The second thing to be watching is what the current generation of models can actually do. And I'll skip the benchmarks, both because they're mostly geared towards coding tasks, and also because they sound like gibberish to normal people. But what you really need to know is that the capabilities of frontier models— so as I'm recording this, it's the highest level, Opus and Fable models on Claude, and five and now six level models on ChatGPT. The capabilities of those models are way beyond what we had access to even a year ago. You can give them whole projects, with all the messy context and the 12 things that have to happen in the right order, and they can work through it all with you, keep track of where you are, and tell you when things get stuck. What that means for you today is, first, that you can ask and expect a lot more of your AI than you probably are right now. And also, that it's really important to have a paid account running the most advanced models. Think of it this way: if you could choose between two advisors or consultants, one with 120 IQ and the other with 150 IQ, for an extra $80 a month, you'd take the second one every time, right? It's a no-brainer, and no pun intended.

The third major change is vibe-coding and agents, which basically means that instead of asking AI a question and getting an answer, you tell AI what you want, and it builds software that can do it, including software that has AI capabilities built right into it. This was clunky and not great until late last year, and then it got good. And over the last few months, it got really, really good, to the point where a coach or consultant with a premium AI subscription can build stuff for themselves or to sell to customers that would have taken massive budgets and serious development teams just a few years ago. And they don't have to know how to write a line of code themselves for it to work. We've been using this capability extensively at Mirasi, replacing expensive commercial tools with homegrown versions that work better for our needs, and also building technology that makes things better and easier for our customers. This is the real frontier for most people today, and if it isn't on your radar, then it deserves a place there soon.

And the last shift you should be paying attention to isn't so much about AI capabilities at all, as it is about how people are using AI to inform their buying decisions. More and more, your prospects are running offers past an AI before they buy. They paste your sales page into Claude or ChatGPT and ask whether they should buy it. And we're seeing this firsthand, people writing in and saying that they bought or didn't buy, because that's what Claude or ChatGPT recommended they do. Which ties into what I was saying earlier about how substance is becoming more important than style. Because not only is style accessible to everyone, but also AIs that are reading your pages don't care about style, even a little. So the takeaway is to double down on substance, and also make sure all the information that an AI might be looking for is clear and available on your pages.

Now, with those four areas to pay attention to, there's also a lot of noise that you can ignore. You don't need to worry about which particular tool and model is ahead this week. The frontier labs are all competing with each other, and it's a red queen race between them. Sometimes one is ahead, sometimes the other, but they're all advancing at pretty similar paces. So choose the platform that's right for you based on things that are more durable, like I did when I switched to Claude earlier this year.
