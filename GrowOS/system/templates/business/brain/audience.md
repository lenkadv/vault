# Audience

> This is who your marketing is for. The clearer this is, the more your posts and
> emails will feel written for one real person instead of everyone. The system
> drafts this during setup and asks you to correct it. You will know your own
> customer the moment you read it.

_Research can add lines here too, each with a mark: **confirmed** (you checked
it), **rough** (close enough, not exact), or **unchecked** (research found it,
nobody has confirmed it yet). Anything unmarked counts as unchecked._

## How much they already know
Someone who has never heard of your kind of solution needs a different first
sentence than someone comparing two providers. Pick where most of your people are:

[PLACEHOLDER: pick one and delete the rest —
  **unaware**: they do not know they have this problem yet
  **problem-aware**: they know it hurts, not that anything can fix it
  **solution-aware**: they know solutions like yours exist, not that you do
  **you-aware**: they know you, and are deciding
  **ready**: they are choosing between you and one other]

[PLACEHOLDER: anything else about where they are when they meet you]

## More than one kind of customer

Add a section for each, with a short name at the top (for example
"## The busy owner" and "## The first-timer"). Keep each one to a real person you
could picture.

Then tell the system how to choose between them, because otherwise it will write
for an average of the two — which is nobody:

**Routing rules**
- [PLACEHOLDER: a rule, for example "anything on LinkedIn is for the busy owner" or "the newsletter is for people who already bought" or "ads always speak to the first-timer"]
- [PLACEHOLDER: another rule, or delete this line]
- **When it is unclear:** [PLACEHOLDER: which one to default to, or "ask me"]

One kind of customer? Delete this whole section — you are done.
