# Email types

A catalog of the shapes an email can take. Each entry says what it is, when
to use it, how it's built, and shows a plain example outline you can fill
with your own words. Some of these are a single email. Others are a short
sequence, because that's how they actually work in real inboxes.

None of these are rules. They are shapes to reach for depending on what the
moment calls for. Mix them, shorten them, and always keep the voice yours.

---

## 1. Newsletter
**What it is:** a regular email that goes out on a rhythm, weekly or
biweekly, with something useful in it — not a pitch.
**When to use:** to stay in front of your list consistently and give people
a real reason to keep opening your email.
**Shape:** one clear idea, told simply, with a short personal note and a
light closing link if there is one. Not five unrelated things crammed in.
**Example:**
> [Hook: a question or observation that opens the idea.]
> [The idea itself — one lesson or one story, in a few short paragraphs.]
> [One practical takeaway they can use today.]
> [Sign-off, plus a P.S. with something extra.]

## 2. Promo
**What it is:** an email built around one specific offer, with a clear ask
and a real reason to act now.
**When to use:** when there is a genuine offer to make — a sale, a limited
spot, a bonus that ends. Not a soft mention buried at the bottom of a value
email.
**Shape:** state what's on offer plainly, say who it's for, give the reason
to act now, and make the next step obvious.
**Example:**
> [What's on offer, in one clear line.]
> [Who it's for and the one problem it solves.]
> [Why now — a real deadline or limit, not manufactured urgency.]
> [The single next step: the link, the button, what to click.]

## 3. Welcome
**What it is:** the first few emails a new subscriber gets, before anything
is asked of them. An introduction, not a pitch.
**When to use:** the moment someone joins your list — right after a signup
or a lead-magnet download.
**Shape:** a short sequence, usually 3 to 5 emails, each with one job:
deliver what was promised, introduce who you are, share your best lesson,
then a first soft mention of what you offer.
**Example:**
> Email 1 (immediately): [Deliver what they signed up for. Say who you are
> in one line.]
> Email 2 (day 1-2): [Your story — why you do this. Build real connection.]
> Email 3 (day 3-5): [Your best lesson or framework. Prove you're worth
> reading.]
> Email 4 (day 7+): [A soft, honest mention of what you offer, if it fits.]

## 4. Nurture
**What it is:** the ongoing rhythm of emails between launches that keeps you
top of mind without constantly selling.
**When to use:** any time you're not actively launching something, to keep
the relationship warm.
**Shape:** rotate through a few honest reasons to email — a lesson, a story,
an observation, a bit of proof — with only an occasional soft mention of
what you offer. Roughly one offer for every five or six value emails.
**Example:**
> [Week 1: a lesson or tip, no pitch.]
> [Week 2: a short story or observation.]
> [Week 3: a small proof point or result.]
> [Week 4: a soft, honest mention of the offer, if it's a natural fit.]

## 5. Launch
**What it is:** a short, intense sequence built around one event — an offer
opening, a doors-open window, a release.
**When to use:** when you have something real to launch and a genuine
window of time to sell it in.
**Shape:** three stages. Warm the list up on the problem before you open
anything, announce and explain plainly once it's open, then close it out
with honest urgency, not manufactured pressure.
**Example:**
> Pre-launch (2-3 emails): [Name the problem. Share what's coming. Build
> real anticipation.]
> Open (2-3 emails): [Announce it. Explain what it is and who it's for.
> Answer the obvious objection.]
> Close (2-3 emails): [The real deadline. A last honest reminder. Then it's
> done — say so.]

## 6. Win-back
**What it is:** a short sequence aimed at subscribers who have gone quiet —
stopped opening, stopped clicking.
**When to use:** when a chunk of your list hasn't engaged in a while and you
want to know whether they're still interested, before you let them go.
**Shape:** a few emails, escalating in directness — first curiosity, then
your best content, then a plain ask, then a clean goodbye if there's no
response.
**Example:**
> Email 1: [It's been a while. Here's what you missed. (curiosity, low
> pressure)]
> Email 2: [Your best recent piece of content, no ask attached.]
> Email 3: [A direct, honest question: still want to hear from me?]
> Email 4: [The last one: say plainly you'll remove them soon unless they
> want to stay.]

## 7. Cart-abandon (ecommerce)
**What it is:** a short sequence triggered when someone adds a product to
their cart and leaves without buying.
**When to use:** for any ecommerce store where checkout can be abandoned —
one of the highest-return sequences a store can run.
**Shape:** a quick reminder first, then a nudge that handles a likely
objection, then, only if it fits the business, a real incentive to finish.
**Example:**
> Email 1 (1 hour later): [A plain reminder — here's what you left, here's
> the link back.]
> Email 2 (next day): [Handle the likely hesitation — shipping, sizing, a
> question they might have. Add a review or a plain reassurance.]
> Email 3 (2-3 days later): [Only if it's a real fit for the business: a
> genuine incentive to finish, stated honestly.]

## 8. Trial-nurture (SaaS)
**What it is:** a sequence that runs during a free trial, aimed at getting
someone to a real result before the trial ends.
**When to use:** for any product with a free trial or a freemium period, to
guide someone toward the moment the product actually proves its worth.
**Shape:** get them to one meaningful action fast, follow up on what they
did or didn't do, show them what they're missing, then make the decision
point clear before time runs out.
**Example:**
> Day 0: [Welcome them in. Point at the one action that gets them to a real
> result fastest.]
> Day 2-3: [Check in based on what they did or didn't do. Remove the
> friction that's likely stopping them.]
> Day 5-6: [Show a result, a feature, or a story that proves the value,
> matched to their actual use.]
> Day 8-9 (trial ending): [Make the decision plain — what happens next, and
> the simple next step.]

---

**One rule for all eight:** whether it's a single email or a sequence, it
still needs one honest reason to land in someone's inbox. If you can't say
in one line why this email earns its place, don't send it yet.
