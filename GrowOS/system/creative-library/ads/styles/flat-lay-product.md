# Messy Desk Flat-Lay

**Description:** An overhead desk photo with your product (or a printed version of a digital guide) as the hero. Messy and real, not styled like a catalog shoot.

**Prompt template:**
```
Overhead iPhone photo of a real messy desk, not styled perfectly. A printed booklet titled "[PRODUCT NAME]" sits slightly crooked near center, one corner dog-eared. A half-drunk oat milk latte in a ceramic mug with a drip stain on the side. A pen with the cap off. A crumpled sticky note nearby. Corner of a laptop with fingerprints visible on the trackpad area. A charging cable snaking across the desk. One earphone bud. The booklet has a small crease on the cover. Warm afternoon light from a window casting long shadows, slightly overexposed highlights. Background objects slightly out of focus. The composition is casual, not centered, like someone snapped this to show a friend. Slight lens vignetting at corners. No watermarks.
```

**Variations:** Adapt the props to match your product's real context (coffee shop vs. home office, etc.).

## Why this works

A messy, off-center desk photo reads as a real person's actual workspace, not a product shoot, which makes a purely digital product feel like something that physically exists and just arrived.

## Audience fit

Buyers who respond better to "this is a real thing" tangibility than to a clean render, useful for guides, workbooks, or any offer that benefits from feeling physical.

## Adapting to your business

Print your actual product name on the booklet cover and swap the props to match your world. Willow & Wren could flat-lay a physical product next to packing tape and a shipping label; Summit Books & Tax could flat-lay a printed client checklist next to a calculator. Keep the imperfect, off-center framing; a perfectly centered flat-lay reads as a stock photo instead of a real desk.
