# Threads

Threads rewards a post that sounds like a real thought said out loud, not a
broadcast. It's a text-first, conversational space, closer to a group chat
anyone can wander into than a stage. The crowd is still finding its shape,
but it responds to a warmer, less combative version of what works on X —
sharp is fine, aggressive is not.

## Format

- **Keep it short.** Threads gives you plenty of room (500 characters), but
  the posts that travel are still tight — say the thing, don't pad it.
- **The first line still has to earn attention**, even without a hard fold.
  People are scrolling fast; give them a reason to stop on line one.
- **Write like you're talking, not announcing.** Threads leans
  conversational — more "here's what I'm thinking" than "here's my hot
  take."
- **Replies are the whole point.** A post that invites a real answer does
  better than one that just states an opinion and walks away.
- **Media is optional, not required.** Plenty of the best-performing posts
  are just text.

## Hooks that fit

See `../hooks.md`. The question hook and the problem named plainly both suit
Threads' conversational tone. Be careful with the bold claim and the
contrarian take — the same line that reads as sharp on X can read as
combative here. Soften the edge before you post it.

## Hashtags and emoji

As a starting point: hashtags are rare here — 0 to 1 at most, only if
genuinely relevant. Emoji used lightly, the same as X, mostly for tone. If
the business's own `brain/voice.md` or `brand.md` says otherwise, that wins.

## What to avoid

- Reposting an X hot take word for word — Threads reads it as louder and
  more aggressive than X does.
- Dead-link posts with no real thought behind them.
- Ignoring replies. This is a conversation platform; walking away from one
  defeats the point.
- Treating it like a press release channel. It isn't one.
