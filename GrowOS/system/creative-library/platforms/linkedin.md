# LinkedIn

LinkedIn rewards a real point made in plain language, not a press release. The
people here are professionals and decision-makers, scrolling in short bursts
between meetings. They save and comment on posts that teach them something or
tell them something true about work, and they scroll past anything that sounds
like a corporate memo.

## Format

- **Length:** 900 to 1500 characters is the sweet spot. Long enough to make a
  real point, short enough to read in one sitting.
- **The first line is everything.** LinkedIn folds your post after about 150
  characters behind "see more." If the first line does not earn the click to
  expand, nobody reads the rest.
- **Short paragraphs.** One or two sentences per line, with a blank line
  between. A wall of text gets scrolled past even if the words are good.
- **End with something to grab onto.** A clear takeaway or a real question.
  Not "Thoughts?" — a question that actually gives someone something to say.
- **Native video beats a link out.** If you post video, look at the camera in
  the first four seconds. That is when most people decide whether to keep
  watching.
- **Quality over quantity.** One or two strong posts a week beats posting
  every day. A steady, real voice earns more than volume.

## Hooks that fit

See `../hooks.md` for the full set. On LinkedIn, the story open, the before
and after, and the mistake hook do the most work — this audience wants a real
lesson from a real situation, not a clever line. Save the bold claim for when
you can actually back it up; this crowd is quick to push back on a claim with
nothing behind it.

## Hashtags and emoji

As a starting point: 3 to 5 hashtags at the bottom of the post, and emoji used
sparingly, mostly to break up a list rather than decorate every line. If the
business's own `brain/voice.md` or `brand.md` says something different,
follow that instead — these numbers are a default, not a rule.

## What to avoid

- Corporate jargon and buzzwords. Say the plain thing.
- A win with no lesson in it. Nobody wants the humble-brag; they want what
  you learned.
- Walls of text with no line breaks.
- Fishing for engagement with "Agree?" or "Repost if..." — ask a real
  question, or don't ask one.
