# Instagram

Instagram rewards a scroll-stopping image or video first, and a caption that
adds to it, not one that carries it alone. The crowd here is broad and
visual: people looking for ideas, inspiration, and things worth saving to
look at again later. A caption with no photo to back it up will not save a
weak post.

## Format

- **The image or video comes first.** Write the caption to support what's on
  screen, not to explain it away. If the caption is doing all the work, the
  visual needs to change.
- **Caption length varies.** A short, punchy caption suits a strong photo. A
  longer, story-style caption works when the topic needs it — Instagram does
  not punish length the way it once did, but the first ~125 characters
  (before "more") still need to hook.
- **Carousels earn more engagement than a single image.** People swipe,
  which counts as extra time spent on the post. Good for a tip list or a
  step-by-step (see `../post-styles.md`).
- **Reels get the widest reach for discovery.** Show a face or motion in the
  first two seconds, or the scroll wins.
- **Use line breaks in longer captions.** Same reason as everywhere else: a
  wall of text loses readers.

## Hooks that fit

See `../hooks.md`. The curiosity gap and the callout both work well here,
because the caption's job is to make someone stop scrolling and actually
read past the photo. The number hook is a natural fit for a carousel — one
tip per slide.

## Hashtags and emoji

As a starting point: 5 to 15 hashtags, mixing a couple of broad ones with a
few smaller, niche ones your actual audience follows. Emoji are welcome
here, used as visual bullets or to add warmth — this platform runs heavier
on both hashtags and emoji than most. If the business's own `brain/voice.md`
or `brand.md` says otherwise, that wins.

## What to avoid

- A caption that ignores what's in the photo or video.
- A wall of hashtags that reads like spam rather than a real caption.
- "Double tap if you agree" and other empty engagement bait.
- Posting a weak visual and hoping the caption saves it. It won't.
