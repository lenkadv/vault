# Facebook

Facebook rewards content that feels like it came from a real person in a
real community, not a brand page. Discovery is back on Facebook: about half
of what people see now comes from accounts they don't follow, so a post has
to earn attention even from strangers. The crowd here skews a little older
and leans on groups, local business, and people they actually know.

## Format

- **Length:** 300 to 800 characters is the medium sweet spot. Enough room
  for context, short enough to read in a feed scroll.
- **Always pair with an image or video.** A link-only post with no visual
  gets buried.
- **Native video and Reels get preferential reach** over a plain text post
  or an outbound link.
- **Casual, warm tone.** More personal than LinkedIn is fine here. Write
  like you're talking to someone in a community group, not announcing
  something.
- **Questions and polls work well** — this audience is comfortable
  commenting.

## Hooks that fit

See `../hooks.md`. The question hook and the callout fit Facebook's
community feel naturally. The story open works well too, especially with a
real photo attached — this crowd responds to something that feels personal
and true.

## Hashtags and emoji

As a starting point: hashtags barely matter on Facebook — use 0 to 2 if at
all. Emoji are fine in moderation, matching a warm, casual tone. If the
business's own `brain/voice.md` or `brand.md` says otherwise, follow that.

## What to avoid

- Dropping a bare link with no context — write a real post around it.
- Ignoring comments; this platform is built on the back-and-forth.
- A tone that reads like a press release instead of a person.
- Skipping the image or video. Text-only posts get far less reach.
