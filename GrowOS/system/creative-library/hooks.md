# Hook patterns

A hook is the first line of a post. It has one job: earn the second line. A weak
hook wastes a great post, because nobody reads past it. A strong hook makes an
ordinary post work.

Below are ten timeless patterns. Each one says when it fits and shows a plain
example. The examples use blanks like `[thing]` so you can drop in your own words.
Pick a pattern, fill it with something true about your business, and read it out
loud. If it sounds like a real person talking, keep it.

---

## 1. The question
**When to use:** you want the reader to feel the problem before you solve it.
**Example:** "What if the reason [task] takes you all week is not you at all?"

## 2. The bold claim
**When to use:** you have a real, defensible point of view and want to plant a flag.
**Example:** "Most advice about [topic] is quietly wrong. Here is what actually works."

## 3. The contrarian take
**When to use:** you disagree with the common wisdom and can back it up.
**Example:** "Everyone says you need [popular thing]. You do not. Here is why."

## 4. The before and after
**When to use:** you can show a clear change, a small win, or a transformation.
**Example:** "Six months ago [old situation]. Today [new situation]. One thing changed."

## 5. The mistake
**When to use:** you want to teach by showing what not to do.
**Example:** "I see [audience] make the same [mistake] over and over. It costs them [cost]."

## 6. The number
**When to use:** you have a list, and you want to set a clear promise up front.
**Example:** "3 small changes to [thing] that make a bigger difference than any big one."

## 7. The story open
**When to use:** you have a real moment that pulls the reader straight in.
**Example:** "The email came in at 11pm. I almost did not open it. I am glad I did."

## 8. The curiosity gap
**When to use:** you can tease a surprising answer without giving it away in line one.
**Example:** "The best [thing] I ever did for [goal] cost nothing and took ten minutes."

## 9. The callout
**When to use:** you want the right person to feel spoken to and the wrong person to scroll on.
**Example:** "If you run [type of business] and [common struggle], this one is for you."

## 10. The problem, named plainly
**When to use:** your audience feels a pain they have never seen put into words.
**Example:** "You are not bad at [task]. You have just never had a system for it."

---

**One rule for all ten:** the hook has to be true. A hook that overpromises earns
the click and loses the trust. Say the real thing in the sharpest honest way.
