# Examples

Three worked rounds, for three invented businesses in three different trades. Every
business, person, number, price and quote in these files is constructed to show the
shape of a good answer. Nothing here comes from a real company or a real customer.

**No sentence in these files may appear in a real output.** Every quoted line names
the invented business it belongs to — Ninebark Press, Ferngate Facilities, Drayhorn
Studio — precisely so that it cannot be lifted. If one of these sentences ever
shows up in real work, it is a copy, and it is wrong. Read them for the shape of
the thinking; write the words from the business's own brain.

They are here for one reason: **they do not look alike.** Read them together before
you write your first offer document, so you do not build every round to the same
mould. The document's shape follows what is doing the work in that round.

- `paid-sample-box.md` — a **shape** round for a two-person letterpress print shop
  selling to couples. The folder is thin: `proof/` is empty and `compliance.md` is
  still template. So the document leads with what can and cannot honestly be
  claimed, and the open decisions sit near the top, not in a footnote. Urgency is
  real here, and the guarantee is left as a decision for the owner.

- `two-buyers-one-ladder.md` — a **sharpen** round for a commercial cleaning firm.
  Diagnosis first, one lever changed, and a long list of what was deliberately left
  alone. Short on purpose. The price does not move. Urgency is honestly absent. It
  also shows the ending where the owner approves the document but says no to
  updating `brain/business.md`.

- `off-the-hourly-rate.md` — a **pricing** round for a two-person technical
  illustration studio. The recommendation is in the first line, the rest is the
  argument for it. It shows the unit tests, an honest placeholder where the ceiling
  should be, a migration path, the owner overruling part of the recommendation, the
  decision log entry, and the completed brain sync with its receipt.

Three things repeat across all three, because they are law and not style: every
number names where it came from, every gap is written as
`[PLACEHOLDER: what is missing]` rather than filled, and nothing is promised that
the business does not already do.
