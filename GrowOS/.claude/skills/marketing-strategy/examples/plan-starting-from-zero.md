# Example: a plan for a business starting from zero

Invented business, invented facts: Example Bike Repair, a one-person mobile bike
repair service, three months old, covering three postcodes. The fake name sits
inside the sentences on purpose. **No sentence from this page may ever appear in a
real plan** — if one does, the fake name travels with it and the mistake is
obvious. Copy the standard, never the words.

Situation: **starting from zero.** Nothing brings work in yet.

Arc: **the chain** — each step starts only once the one before it is real. Chosen
because a business with five customers has no evidence to build a bigger commitment
on, so every later step has to be paid for by a result rather than a guess. It is
also why there are no month-one, month-two, month-three headings: the steps are
unlocked by results, not by the calendar.

The chain arc decides the ending too, so this page finishes on the step that has
not started yet. The refusals sit in the middle, the watched number is folded into
the outcome, and the checkpoint lives inside the step it gates rather than in a
closing block. Compare that with how `plan-capacity-constrained.md` ends.

This is what would be written into `brain/plan.md`. It runs to about 475 words.

# Marketing plan — Example Bike Repair, now to 31 October

**Situation: starting from zero.** Nothing brings work into Example Bike Repair
yet, so this plan is a chain: each step starts only once the one before it is real.

**The one outcome.** 24 paid Example Bike Repair jobs booked by 31 October, counted
in the booking app on 1 November. Today: 3 in the last 30 days. That weekly count is
the only number we watch.

**Who it is for.** People who commute by bike inside our three postcodes and cannot
easily get a bike to a shop: a flat with no lift, no car, a bike that will not go on
a bus. Not road racers chasing race-day builds. Not anyone outside the three
postcodes. All five Example Bike Repair customers so far were flat-dwelling
commuters (job notes, June and July).

**What we say.** "Example Bike Repair fixes your bike where it is parked, so you
never carry it anywhere." Both shops in `competitors.md` work from fixed premises
with a drop-off window, so neither could say that without changing their business.

**What Example Bike Repair is not doing.** No paid ads: there is no page to send
anyone to, and nobody free to watch a daily budget. No selling bikes: it ties up
the cash we need for tools.

**Step 1 — the two office bike sheds, starting now.** A card in both sheds, plus one
visit each Wednesday morning while people arrive. Doer: the owner, one hour a week
out of the four they said they have. Why here: all five customers so far keep their
bike in a shed like these (job notes), and the owner is on that estate on Wednesdays
anyway.

**Step 2 — the residents' group chat, only if step 1 books 8 jobs by 5 September.**
One short post a fortnight showing a real repair. Doer: the owner, 20 minutes a
fortnight, taking the week to about 1 hour 20. Why here: three of the five had seen
the Example Bike Repair van mentioned in that chat. At five customers that is a bet;
step 1 confirms it. **5 September is the only date here that decides anything: if
the 8 jobs are not there, nothing new opens and we look again at the card and the
Wednesday pitch.**

**Step 3 — a Saturday stall at the weekend market, only if steps 1 and 2 together
book 16 jobs by 30 September.** Doer: the owner, four hours a Saturday. Not started
before then: four hours is the whole weekend, and it is the only line here that
would cost Example Bike Repair a day off.

## What this example shows

- The situation and the arc are both named, and the arc has a reason behind it that
  is about this business, not about plans in general.
- **The arc decides the ending too.** The page finishes on the step that has not
  started, because that is how a chain ends — not on a closing checkpoint block.
- The target has a number, a unit, a date, and where it will be seen, plus the
  baseline it is measured from, from a named source.
- Later steps are conditional, not scheduled. Each says "only if X by <date>".
- The checkpoint is a **stop rule** — if the jobs are not there, nothing new opens —
  and it sits inside the step it gates. Another business would need a different
  logic, in a different place on the page.
- **Two refusals, because two is what this folder supports.** Not three, not five.
  A refusal count that repeats from plan to plan is shape, not thinking.
- The hours note is a **sum**, because this owner stated their hours.
- One watched number, folded into the outcome rather than given its own section.
- The one missing fact uses the exact `[PLACEHOLDER: ...]` token, in three parts:
  what is missing, why it matters here, and what would settle it.
