# Strategy methods

Methods are lenses, not templates. Select the smallest mix that covers the round's actual jobs.

## Selection sequence

1. Identify the audience's awareness stage and the category's sophistication.
2. Identify the strongest available evidence: demonstration, proof, authority, mechanism, offer, or
   lived experience.
3. Identify the emotional job: relief, control, status, belonging, possibility, certainty, speed,
   savings, or avoidance of loss.
4. Identify what can be shown convincingly in the available format.
5. Select two or three methods that make meaningfully different ads.

## Method menu

### Awareness and sophistication

Use when the audience ranges from unaware to most-aware, the category is crowded, or familiar
claims have lost force.

- Unaware: dramatize a recognizable life or work situation without naming a category too early.
- Problem-aware: name the felt problem more accurately than the buyer can.
- Solution-aware: explain why the common solution class disappoints and what must be different.
- Product-aware: prove the mechanism, fit, or objection answer.
- Most-aware: make the offer, reason to act, risk reversal, and next step unmistakable.

This lens is distilled from Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* and Michael Masterson and
John Forde's *Great Leads*. Use the concepts, not copied passages or canned lead formulas.

### Mental movie

Use when identity, emotion, relief, or a desired life can be shown in a concrete moment. Open
inside the scene. Give it a clock, place, action, tension, and turn. The product enters as the
cause of the change, not as a floating claim.

This lens is distilled from Roy H. Williams' *The Wizard of Ads*. Strong for broad human desires,
weak when the scene narrows the audience to an arbitrary, oddly specific trade.

### Offer-first direct response

Use when the offer itself is unusually strong, the audience is warm or most-aware, and the price,
bonus, guarantee, availability, or next step does real selling work. Lead with the offer only when
it is more compelling than the story around it.

This lens draws on Claude Hopkins' *Scientific Advertising*, John Caples' *Tested Advertising
Methods*, and Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers*. Keep claims traceable and avoid manufactured urgency.

### Demonstration and mechanism

Use when the product can be shown doing the work, the buyer doubts how it functions, or the
category is full of vague promises. Show input, action, and output. A real interface, workflow,
teardown, screen recording, physical demonstration, or concrete artifact beats a diagram that
merely labels benefits.

Avoid fake interfaces, unreadable screens, placeholder dashboards, and prompts presented as the
whole mechanism.

### Proof-led

Use when credible evidence is the main barrier. Build around an approved quote, a consented
customer asset, a verified result, a real before-and-after state, or documented authority. The
proof should carry the claim rather than decorate it.

Do not generate a customer's face, edit the meaning of a quote, or pair sensitive outcomes with
aggressive timeframes. Local compliance rules may be stricter than Meta's baseline.

### Native conversation

Use when the message becomes more believable as a feed post, message thread, note, review,
comment, notification, or founder observation. The surface must look authentic and the carrier
identity must be truthful.

Native does not mean fake engagement. Never invent handles, timestamps that imply a real event,
likes, views, comments, or conversations.

### Creator or UGC

Use when a person can embody the buyer, demonstrate the product, answer an objection, or tell the
story more naturally than a designed ad. The first three seconds must establish who it is for and
why to care. Produce for sound-on Reels while staying understandable with captions and no audio.

Avoid generic testimonial delivery, fake spontaneity, and a performer who signals the wrong
audience.

### Contrarian reframe

Use when the buyer holds a costly assumption and the business can replace it with a more useful,
provable model. State the familiar belief, expose the missing distinction, and give a concrete next
step.

Contrarian does not mean insulting the buyer or manufacturing an enemy.

### Salient visual idea

Use when the concept can communicate through one memorable visual act, juxtaposition, or proudly
artificial world. It should remain readable without a paragraph of explanation. This lens borrows
from David Ogilvy's *Ogilvy on Advertising* discipline of research and specificity, and Luke
Sullivan's *Hey Whipple, Squeeze This* emphasis on a real creative idea.

Avoid empty symbolism, generic objects, surrealism without a buyer insight, and polish that makes
the ad feel like a corporate poster.

## Recommendation matrix

| Situation | Lead method | Useful companion |
|---|---|---|
| New category, low awareness | mental movie | awareness and sophistication |
| Crowded category, familiar claims | contrarian reframe | mechanism or proof-led |
| Product is visually demonstrable | demonstration and mechanism | creator or UGC |
| Warm retargeting | offer-first | proof-led or native conversation |
| Trust is the main barrier | proof-led | native conversation |
| Founder-led expertise | native conversation | demonstration or contrarian |
| Emotional service or identity offer | mental movie | proof-led |
| No history and little proof | awareness lens | mechanism plus exploratory visual ideas |
| Established account with known winners | adjacent iteration | one or two exploration methods |

## Portfolio architecture

Tag each selected concept with:

- `awareness`
- `motivation`
- `method`
- `message`
- `narrative`
- `appearance`
- `format`
- `world`: the 2 to 4 word visual scene, surface, or composition. A real diversity axis on its
  own, separate from message or angle.
- `lane`: signal, adjacent, or explore

Two concepts are near-duplicates when the same buyer receives the same reason to believe through
the same narrative and the same visual world. Changing a hook or a background color does not
rescue the pair; `references/concepting.md` gives the operational test for this.

Use local posture rules from `brain/ads/config.md` when they exist. Otherwise:

- With no winners yet, lean toward exploration while keeping one clear offer or mechanism anchor.
- With reliable winners, protect roughly half the round for signal and adjacent ideas, and half for
  new messages, narratives, or appearances.
- During fatigue, keep the underlying desire if it still converts, but retire the repeated hook,
  scene, composition, and carrier.
