# Playbook: the debrief, the interview, and the plan

This covers Steps 6 and 7 of the skill. By the time you reach here, the burst has
run, the drop folder is processed, and the brain already has research written
into it with honest marks. This playbook covers the one condensed debrief that
replaces the old file-by-file correction, the small number of questions worth
asking out loud, and how to turn all of it into `brain/plan.md`.

**The question policy (D16), throughout both:**

- **If research could have answered it, research must have tried first.** Never
  ask the owner something the burst or the site read could have found.
- **Closed choices go through the question tool** where the platform has one
  (AskUserQuestion in Claude Code), with short labels so a pick is a glance, not
  a read.
- **Open questions are plain text, one at a time.** Never two questions in the
  same breath, even when they feel related.
- **No question tool available?** Plain text, one question at a time, same
  rules. Say so once if it changes how the conversation feels, then carry on.
- **One narrow exception: a bundle of short factual fields.** Numbers, names,
  and frequencies (list size, posting frequency, a target number) may be
  asked as one labeled list in a single message — three tiny fields split
  into three separate messages is more friction, not less. Everything
  conversational still stays one question per message. Skip any field
  research already answered; do not re-ask it as part of the bundle.

---

## 1. The condensed debrief (Step 6, D5)

This replaces walking `business.md`, `audience.md`, `voice.md`, and `brand.md`
one file at a time. Instead, one tight pass, two minutes, once:

**"Here's what I found, including what you never told me."** Say it plainly:
what the research turned up that they never said themselves (a review calling
out something they never mention, a competitor gap that points at a real angle,
a pattern in how customers describe the problem). This is the moment that
proves the research actually happened, not a formality before the real work.

Then the **five things you are least sure of**: pick the five findings that are
both consequential (they might show up in a post, a price, a claim about the
business) and low-confidence (marked unchecked or rough, or genuinely
conflicting between sources). Typical candidates: a price you saw on one page
but not confirmed elsewhere, a claim a competitor makes that might also be true
of this business, an audience guess built from thin evidence. List the five,
plainly, and ask the owner to confirm or fix each one. This is the whole
correction moment; there is no separate file-by-file walkthrough after it.

Also name any proof filed as `pending` during setup — the drop-folder pass in
`playbooks/self-gather.md` §5 may have landed one or more. Read each one back
in plain words and get the owner's word on it (real, and theirs to use
publicly, or not) before the plan is written.

Apply their answers directly to the brain files, upgrading marks where they
confirm and correcting the text where they fix. Log anything genuinely
meaningful (a repositioning, a dropped claim) in `brain/decisions.md` with the
why. When any of the five stay open (the owner could not say yes or no on the
spot), leave each one flagged exactly where it already lives — its mark stays
**unchecked** or **rough**, never quietly upgraded — and carry the open list
forward into the close (Step 11).

*Checkpoint (phase: `debriefed`) once this pass is done, even if a couple of the
five are still open.*

## 2. The human-only questions

Ask only what research could not have answered. This is short by design.

### Quick path (three questions)

1. **The bottleneck:** **"What is the one thing keeping your business from
   growing right now?"** Their answer anchors the plan's focus. "Nobody knows we
   exist" leans the plan on reach and content. "People visit but don't buy"
   leans on the offer and the page. "I have no time" leans on a small, doable
   rhythm.
2. **Weekly time:** "How much time a week do you honestly have for this?" Size
   the plan's rhythm to fit; most owners have only a few hours.
3. **A 60-day goal:** "What would a good result look like in the next couple of
   months?" (more leads, more sales of one offer, a growing list, more
   awareness)

### In-depth path adds five more

- **Why people pick them:** "When someone picks you over the alternative, what
  makes them choose you?" Sharpens `business.md` and the plan's angle. If the
  research already surfaced a clear answer, confirm it here instead of asking
  from scratch.
- **Best customer:** "Think of your favorite customer ever. What were they
  like, and how did they find you?" Sharpens `audience.md` and channel choice.
- **Where their week actually goes (open):** "Walk me through a normal week.
  Where does the time actually go?" This is the one genuinely open question in
  the set; let them talk, and pull out anything that shapes the plan's rhythm
  or points at a seasonal pattern worth noting in `plan.md`.
- **Real numbers:** list size, how often they email, and a 90-day number they
  would actually track (subscribers, bookings, revenue, whatever fits).
  Ask this as one question with a short list, not three separate ones. These
  fill the placeholder classes that used to sit blank in the plan: list size,
  posting or email frequency, and the 90-day target.
- **What they never say:** "Anything you never want claimed, promised, or said
  in your marketing?" Write it into `business.md` under "what we will not say."

Stop when the answers stop sharpening the plan. More questions past that point
is friction, not value. Founder-story capture stays out of this interview; that
belongs to a deeper session later, not the first hour.

*Checkpoint (phase: `debriefed`) covers this section too if it was not already
written after section 1.*

## 3. Write the plan (Step 7)

Fill `brain/plan.md` (the template is already there), fed by the dossiers and the
interview, not from a blank page:

- **What we sell and who it is for** — pulled from the corrected `business.md`
  and `audience.md`.
- **Where we are today** — the numbers from the In-depth interview (list size,
  frequency, recent activity) or, on Quick, whatever the research turned up,
  marked rough where it is not owner-confirmed.
- **The one thing to fix right now** — the bottleneck answer, in their words.
- **Where we want to be in 90 days** — the 60-day or 90-day goal they gave you,
  turned into one specific, checkable target.
- **Focus channels and why** — one or two places to show up, chosen from where
  the research showed their audience actually is, and where they already have
  an edge.
- **The weekly rhythm** — sized to the time they told you they have.
- **This month's themes** — the few big ideas everything this month ladders up
  to. Three is plenty.
- **The shape of the year** — fill this from what the "where your week goes"
  answer or the research turned up about busy and quiet periods. If nothing
  points at a real pattern, write "steady all year" rather than guess at one.

The four placeholder classes that used to sit blank (list size, posting
frequency, a 90-day number, seasonality) should be genuinely filled by the
In-depth interview, or, on the Quick path, marked rough from whatever the
research found rather than left as a bare placeholder.

Then show it and let them steer it. It is their file:

- Read it back plainly and ask "does this feel right, or should we shift the
  focus?"
- Apply their edits. Log anything meaningful in `brain/decisions.md` with the
  why.
- Get their nod. This is the plan approval, done live, and it is the green
  light for whatever they choose to make first in Step 9.

*Checkpoint (phase: `plan-written`).*

Do not over-plan. One clear page they will actually follow beats a detailed plan
they never open.
