# Example: one lever, and everything else left alone

**Constructed. Not a real business.** Ferngate Facilities cleans offices and
clinics in one city, six staff, contracts billed monthly. The owner opened with a
pricing question: *"I think we are too expensive — should we drop the middle tier?"*

**No sentence in this file may appear in a real output.** Every quoted line below
names Ferngate Facilities on purpose, so if one of them ever turns up in real work
it is a copy, and it is wrong.

## What was said back first

The question was answered, and the binding thing was named before it:

> I will answer that, and first: nothing in the Ferngate folder says the price is
> wrong. Eight of Ferngate's last ten quotes closed at a discount, and seven of
> those eight were discounted for the same stated reason — the customer wanted the
> clinical paperwork that only sits in the top Ferngate tier. That is not a price
> problem wearing a price costume. The three Ferngate tiers were built from a
> feature list, and two different buyers are being pushed up and down one ladder.

The record that claim rests on: the ten most recent quote files in `work/`, plus
the owner's own account of why each discount was given, recorded as their statement
with today's date.

## The document (shape follows the diagnosis: one lever, then the leave-alone list)

---

**Headline:** Split the ladder into two packages — one lever

### The binding weakness

Two genuinely different buyers are being sold one three-step ladder.

- **Office managers** buy a clean office. They care about evenings, keys, and not
  having to think about it.
- **Practice managers** at dental and physio clinics buy a documented, signed-off
  clean they can show an inspector. They care about the paperwork.

Neither wants more of the other's thing. That is the test tiers have to pass, and
these do not: the middle tier's buyer and problem cannot be written in one sentence
without using the word "medium".

Evidence: `audience.md` names both groups and records the clinic objection —
"I need to be able to prove it was done." Eight of the last ten quotes were
discounted; seven of those for the clinical paperwork specifically.

### The one change

Stop selling three tiers. Sell two packages.

| Package | Who it is for | The problem it solves |
|---|---|---|
| **Ferngate office contract** | The person who manages a small office | The office is clean when people arrive, and nobody has to organise it |
| **Ferngate clinic contract** | The practice manager at a dental, physio or podiatry practice | The clean is done to a written schedule and signed off, so it can be shown to an inspector |

Same work, same people, same rates. What changes is that a clinic no longer has to
buy an office package and then argue its way up.

**Shippable wording — Ferngate office contract.**
> Your office cleaned after hours by the same two Ferngate cleaners every week, to
> a schedule you set. Keys held under a signed Ferngate key agreement. One named
> contact here, not a call centre.

**Shippable wording — Ferngate clinic contract.**
> A clinical clean from Ferngate to a written schedule, signed off after every
> visit, with the record kept for you. The same Ferngate team every week, all of
> them background-checked and trained on that schedule.

`compliance.md` was checked before that second block was written. It says any
wording about clinical standards must be approved by the owner before use, so the
clinic wording is marked as needing your sign-off, and no claim about meeting a
named standard appears in it.

### What to watch, and when

The next ten quotes. How many close without a discount. Look again when the tenth
one is done, not on a date.

### Left alone on purpose

- **The price.** Nothing in the record says the number is wrong. Nobody has walked
  away on price alone in the last ten quotes; they walked away, or negotiated,
  because of what was in the box. Changing the number now would make it impossible
  to tell which change did anything.
- **The promise wording for offices.** It is working. It is unchanged.
- **The guarantee.** Unchanged, and it stays "no money-back guarantee" for the same
  reason as before: once the crew has worked the evening, the hours are spent. What
  you stand behind is unchanged too — anything damaged is repaired or replaced
  under your insurance, and a missed visit is made up within 48 hours or not
  charged for. That resolves honestly and does not need touching.
- **Urgency.** There is none, and there should not be. Nothing in the folder gives
  a real reason for a customer to sign this month rather than next, and a
  manufactured one would cost you more trust than it earns. What would create a
  real one: a genuine limit on how many contracts the current six staff can take.
  That number is not written down anywhere. `[PLACEHOLDER: how many contracts your
  current team can hold before you have to hire — it would make a real capacity
  line possible.]`
- **What is included.** Unchanged in both packages. Every line already had a job.

### The discount rules, from here

Three reasons, and no others:

1. More sites on one contract.
2. A twelve-month commitment rather than rolling.
3. Two services on one site.

Anything else is a no. A discount given for the paperwork is not a discount, it is
the ladder failing, and this round is meant to end that.

### Proof

`proof/` holds four entries. Two are approved for use; both are from office
customers. Neither mentions the clinical paperwork, so the clinic package ships
with no proof and that is stated on the page rather than filled with an office
quote used out of place. What would change it: one clinic customer agreeing to be
quoted.

---

## How the round ended

The owner approved the document. When shown the exact before-and-after for the
offer ladder in `brain/business.md`, they said not yet — they wanted to run the new
wording past their clinic customers first.

So the item stayed at `approved`, and the receipt recorded the truth:

```yaml
publish_destination: brain/business.md
publish_ref: ""
publish_attempted_at: 2026-07-30T15:20:00.000Z
publish_state: waiting-owner
publish_note: Document approved. Owner asked to leave brain/business.md unchanged until they have spoken to clinic customers.
published_at: ""
```

And they were told, in one line, what that means: the offer is decided but the
brain still says three tiers, so anything else GrowOS writes will keep using the
old ladder until they say go.

## Why this passes the bar

One binding weakness, named, resting on the last ten quotes and a recorded
objection. Exactly one lever changed. Everything else listed as unchanged, with a
reason each. One observable thing to watch. Urgency resolved as an honest absence
plus what would create a real one. The compliance file was opened before clinical
wording was written. Proof gated, and the clinic package honestly ships without
any.

Notice what is not here: no new guarantee, no bonus, no launch discount, no
rewritten promise. The owner asked about price and the price did not move, because
nothing in the record said it should.
