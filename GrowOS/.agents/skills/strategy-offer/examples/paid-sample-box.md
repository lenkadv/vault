# Example: shape a first offer on a thin folder

**Constructed. Not a real business.** Ninebark Press is two people and one press,
making letterpress wedding invitations to order and selling direct to couples
online. The owner asked for "something people can buy before they commit to a whole
order".

**No sentence in this file may appear in a real output.** Every quoted line below
names Ninebark Press on purpose, so if one of them ever turns up in real work it is
a copy, and it is wrong.

## The folder they had

- `business.md`: filled in. One press. A full invitation suite is quoted from £680.
  They take at most 24 full suites between March and September because the press
  cannot run more than that.
- `audience.md`: filled in. The recorded objection, in a couple's own words: "the
  photos look lovely but I have no idea what the paper actually feels like."
- `methodology.md`: filled in. They mix ink by hand to match a colour, and they
  print a test sheet on the real stock before every run.
- `competitors.md`: three entries, all recorded eleven months ago.
- `proof/`: empty.
- `compliance.md`: still template.
- `decisions.md`: one entry from last year — they stopped offering digital printing
  because it was not what they wanted to be known for.

## What was said back first, before any questions

The read of the situation and the draft, together. One question was asked
afterwards, not before: *"What do you already do on every job that you have never
charged for?"* — because the answer decides whether there is a real component here
or only adjectives. The owner's answer: the hand-mixed ink match and the test sheet.

## The document (shape follows the thin folder: gaps first)

---

**Headline:** Paid sample box — first shape

### What we can honestly say right now

`proof/` is empty, so this document contains no customer results, no testimonials
and no numbers about outcomes. Every claim below is marked.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| "Ninebark mixes ink by hand to match your colour" | Supportable — `methodology.md` |
| "Ninebark prints a test sheet on your real stock before the run" | Supportable — `methodology.md` |
| "Ninebark takes at most 24 suites a season" | Supportable — `business.md` |
| "Couples who see Ninebark paper first order with more confidence" | Not supportable — nothing in the folder. Cut from the page wording. |
| "Ninebark paper is better than what you will get elsewhere" | Not supportable — `competitors.md` records what three others offer but nothing about paper, and it is eleven months old. Cut. |

### The open decisions (these need you)

- `[PLACEHOLDER: whether a couple who dislikes the box can send it back for a
  refund, and what that costs us if it happens. Nothing in the folder says, and it
  changes the guarantee section below.]`
- `[PLACEHOLDER: the box price. `business.md` holds the suite price but no sample
  price, and I am not going to invent one. Bracketing it needs two numbers you
  have and I do not: what one box costs you in stock, ink and press time, and what
  a couple currently spends elsewhere trying to solve this — usually ordering
  single samples from several printers.]`
- `compliance.md` is still template, so the guarantee wording below has not been
  checked against anything. It needs your confirmation before it goes on a page.

### The promise

> Four sheets of the paper Ninebark would actually print your invitations on,
> inked in a colour Ninebark mixes to match yours, posted to you. You hold the real
> thing before you spend anything on a full order. It is not a design — it is
> Ninebark paper, ink and lettering, so you can feel what you are buying.

Buyer: a couple choosing a printer. Outcome: they hold the real paper and ink
before committing. Boundary: not a design, not a proof of their invitation.

### Who it is for, and who it is not for

**For:** couples who have found you online, like the photographs, and cannot judge
paper from a screen. That hesitation is recorded word for word in `audience.md`.

**Not for:** anyone who needs invitations inside four weeks. The press schedule in
`business.md` means a box plus a full run does not fit in that window, and taking
their money would set up a promise you cannot keep. Say so and point them to a
digital printer.

### What is in it, and the job each piece does

| Piece | The obstacle it removes |
|---|---|
| Four sheets of the real stock | "I cannot tell what the paper feels like" — the recorded objection |
| Ink mixed to their colour | Turns an abstract colour choice into something they can hold |
| The test sheet from a recent run | Shows the depth of the impression, which does not photograph |
| A note of what a full suite would cost for their guest count | Removes the "I will have to ask" step before they can decide |

Nothing else. No extras were added to make the box look fuller, and there is no
right number of things to include.

### What makes it a decision, not an adjective

Hand-mixed ink to match a colour, and a test sheet printed on the real stock before
every run. Both come from `methodology.md`, both are things you already do, and
both are choices a buyer can weigh. Neither is claimed as unique — `competitors.md`
is eleven months old and does not record their process, so "nobody else does this"
would be an invention.

### The guarantee

Unresolved on purpose. See the placeholder above: whether a box can be returned for
a refund is your call, and `compliance.md` has never been filled in. What can be
said today without deciding anything: the box price comes off a full order placed
within 90 days. That is a real reduction in risk for the buyer and it costs you
only the box.

### Urgency

Real, and it traces to a fact: 24 suites a season, from `business.md`, and the
season runs March to September. The honest way to say it is the fact itself, not a
countdown:

> Ninebark prints 24 suites a season. Once those are booked, the next Ninebark
> date is the following spring.

No timer, no "only three left" unless the booking sheet actually says three.

### Shippable wording

**Promise line.** "Hold the Ninebark paper before you spend a penny on the
invitations. Four sheets of your real stock, inked in your colour, posted to you."

**Risk line.** "The Ninebark box price comes off your suite if you order within 90
days."

**Price line.** `[PLACEHOLDER: the box price — see the open decision above.]`

**Name.** Three options, plainest first: **Ninebark sample box** · Ninebark paper
and ink box · The First Sheet. Recommend **Ninebark sample box**, because it is
what a couple would already type into a search bar.

### Where the words get said out loud first

The next five enquiry emails. Reply with the promise line as written and see
whether anyone asks a follow-up question about the box, rather than asking again
about paper.

### What we left out

No comparison to another printer, because `competitors.md` is eleven months old and
holds nothing about their paper. No "couples love it" line, because `proof/` is
empty. Both become possible the moment there is something real to say.

---

## Why this passes the bar

The promise has a buyer, an outcome and a boundary. There is a not-for line with a
reason. Every included piece has a job and there are only four of them. There is a
real component from `methodology.md` and no claim that it is unique. The guarantee
resolves as a named decision for the owner, and the template `compliance.md` is
said once. Urgency is real and traced. Every claim is marked against an empty
`proof/`, which is a pass. Nothing in it obliges the shop to do anything it does
not already do.

Two numbers are missing and both are placeholders rather than guesses. That is what
a thin folder is supposed to look like.
