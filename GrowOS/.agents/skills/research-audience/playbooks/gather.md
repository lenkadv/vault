# Playbook: gather

Read `SKILL.md` first. Its law governs everything below: only confirmed sources,
the two-mode split, honest degradation, and the depth rule for Quick versus
In-depth.

## Mode 1 — their own material

The strongest evidence there is, because it is this business's real customers,
talking without you in the room.

Look in, in order:

1. `add-to-brain/`, if it exists (the standing drop folder the owner fills
   during onboarding and any time after — transcripts, support-email exports,
   survey results, call notes, PDFs).
2. `brain/inbox/` — anything dropped there that has not yet been filed as
   research.
3. Anything pasted directly into the conversation, when you are running
   standalone and the owner hands you something on the spot.

For each real item, extract, with a citation back to the item:

- **Jobs-to-be-done** — functional (the task they are trying to get done),
  emotional (how they want to feel, or stop feeling), social (how they want to
  be seen).
- **Unprompted pains** — a complaint or frustration that surfaces on its own,
  not in answer to a question you or a survey asked.
- **Trigger events** — the moment that made them start looking for a fix
  ("after the third late shipment", "when my old provider raised prices").
- **Desired outcomes** — the result they picture, in their own words, not the
  feature that gets them there.
- **Exact vocabulary** — the words and phrases they reach for. Steal these
  verbatim; do not tidy them into more polished language. A customer's own
  clumsy phrase is worth more to a future draft than a smooth paraphrase.
- **Alternatives considered** — what they tried, weighed, or almost bought
  instead.

A support email that is really about a billing question, not the problem the
business solves, is not audience evidence. Skip it rather than stretch it.

## Mode 2 — watering holes

Confirmed review pages, communities, comment sections, social replies. Every
source here must already be confirmed (see `SKILL.md`, "Only read sources that
are confirmed") — domain-linked automatically, name-searched only once the
owner has said yes.

Route the source set by business type. This is a starting point, not a fence:
use what actually fits, and say in the dossier which sources you actually
checked.

| Business type | Likely watering holes |
|---|---|
| Local / in-person | Google Maps reviews, Yelp, Facebook page reviews, a local Nextdoor or community group if confirmed |
| SaaS / software | G2, Capterra, Reddit threads about the category, the product's own public support forum |
| E-commerce | Amazon reviews (if they sell there), Trustpilot, Instagram or TikTok comments on their own posts |
| Info products / coaching | Course-platform reviews, a Facebook or Discord community if confirmed, YouTube comments on their own videos, podcast reviews if they host or guest on one |

For each item you read, capture:

- **Verbatim quote** — copied exactly, never reworded.
- **Where** — the site and, where the platform shows it, a link or a way to
  find it again.
- **Sentiment** — what they were feeling when they wrote it (frustrated,
  relieved, disappointed, delighted).
- **Theme** — which of the dossier's nine sections this belongs to (a pain, an
  objection, a desired transformation, and so on).

Stop reading a source once the same handful of themes keep repeating with
nothing new — that repetition is the signal you have reached saturation for a
Quick pass. In-depth keeps going past the first repetition, specifically to
give any thin segment a second look.

## Handling a candidate you cannot confirm mid-run

If, partway through, you turn up a source that looks worth reading but was not
in the confirmed list (a review site you stumbled on, a community the owner
never mentioned), do not read it. Note its name and where you found it. It
goes in the dossier's "Sources found but not read" line, for the owner to
confirm on a later pass. This is not a failure to report quietly; it is a
normal finding, and it is often useful on its own (it tells the owner where
their customers are talking that they had not thought to check).

## When there is nothing to read

An empty inbox and no confirmed Mode 2 sources yet is a real, common state,
especially on a brand-new install. Say so in one line, and either wait for the
owner to fill `add-to-brain/` (during onboarding, this is expected — you may
be dispatched again once it fills) or write the smallest honest dossier you
can: what you looked for, what you found, and that the file is thin because
there is not yet material to draw from. A thin dossier that says so plainly is
a correct dossier. An invented one is not.
