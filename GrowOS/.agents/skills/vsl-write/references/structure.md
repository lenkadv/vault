# VSL structure — the seven-part canon

This is mined from the owner's own real, shipped VSL work — not a generic
VSL-course template. Seven parts, not the ten-beat pattern (hook, problem,
agitation, solution-intro, proof, product-reveal, offer-stack, objections,
guarantee, close) an older version of this skill used. There is no
standalone Objection Handling section and no standalone Guarantee section
anywhere below: objections get woven into MECHANISM as loops, and the
guarantee lives inside CLOSE. If a draft grows either one back as its own
block, fold it back in.

**On the examples below.** Every line marked "Example" is a real line from
the mined script, kept only where it names no product, price, or person —
it exists to show the MOVE, never to be copied into a draft. Where the real
line could not be shown without leaking a name, a price, or a persona
detail, the illustration is a bracketed shape instead: fill it with THIS
business's own true material, never with a number borrowed from this file.
See "What generalizes" at the end.

## Timing is a shape, not a stopwatch

Only one complete real script exists to measure, so what follows is a set of
DEFAULT proportions — starting points, adjusted to the business and the
offer, not a rule to enforce.

**Default total length:** 7-8 minutes. At 160 words a minute (the safe
default for a runtime estimate; treat 170 as the top of a normal range, not
the target), that is roughly 1,200-1,400 words. Propose this when the owner
has no length preference (SKILL.md Phase 0).

**The length bends around the idea, not the other way round.** The one real
script measured ran to about 1,420 words — past its own stated ceiling —
because MECHANISM needed the room once the idea was actually on the page.
Treat the total as a starting point. If cutting MECHANISM to hit a word
count would blur the one idea the whole video hangs on, let the idea keep
the room and say so, rather than truncating it to make a number.

**The proportions, and how to scale them.** Multiply the total seconds by a
part's share for its runtime; multiply the total words by the same share for
its word budget. Worked example at the 8-minute default (480 seconds,
~1,280 words at 160 wpm):

| # | Part | Share | ~Seconds of 8:00 | ~Words of ~1,280 |
|---|------|------:|------:|------:|
| 1 | CAPTURE | 6% | 29s | 77 |
| 2 | CONNECT | 16% | 77s | 205 |
| 3 | AGITATE | 13% | 62s | 166 |
| 4 | MECHANISM | 26% | 125s | 333 |
| 5 | PROOF | 10% | 48s | 128 |
| 6 | OFFER | 13% | 62s | 166 |
| 7 | CLOSE | 16% | 77s | 205 |

MECHANISM is the biggest block on purpose — wider than PROOF and OFFER
combined. That is not a rounding accident; see the Big Domino rule below.

## 1. CAPTURE — the hook

**Share:** ~6% of runtime.
**Job:** Pattern-interrupt, then open a curiosity loop. Never open with a
name, a greeting, or a credential.
**Opens with:** Cold. No "Hi, I'm ___." A contrarian claim or question lands
in the first line.
**Hands to CONNECT via:** an explicit verbal bridge — something like "So
here's a little context" — never a header.

**Technique — three hooks, one filmed.** Write three variants, each a
different rhetorical pattern, each with its own filmable visual concept so a
swap later costs nothing:

1. **Visual analogy** — the claim is made through a picture, not an
   argument.
2. **Curiosity-contrarian** — the common question or belief, named, then
   overturned in the same breath.
3. **Show-then-explain** — open on the result or the demo itself, explain
   what it was after the fact.

Present all three, mark which one you would film first, and keep the other
two in the shot list (`references/production.md`) rather than discarding
them. If the business has a strong past VSL or hook in `brain/samples/`, use
that as the benchmark for "does this sound like them" — never a hardcoded
outside example.

**Example** (real, kept because it names no product, price, or person):
*"Everyone's asking the same question about [X] right now. '[The obvious
question people keep asking].' Wrong question."* The specific question is
this source's own; the move — name the common question, then overturn it in
one word — is what generalizes.

**Honesty rule:** a contrarian claim has to be one this business can
actually back up later in MECHANISM. A hook that promises a reveal the
script never delivers is a broken promise, not a pattern-interrupt.

**Scaling note:** CAPTURE's 6% share is a ceiling, not a target. A real
pattern-interrupt usually lands in 25-45 words whatever the total runtime
is — at shorter lengths the percentage overstates it. Write the hook as
short as the interrupt needs and give the spare seconds to MECHANISM.

## 2. CONNECT — credibility, fast

**Share:** ~16% of runtime.
**Job:** Build rapport before credentials, and land the credentials almost
in passing. The "reluctant hero" move: not a resume, not a sob story —
closer to "yeah, I get it," with room for self-deprecating humor.
**Opens with:** the verbal bridge from CAPTURE — "So here's a little
context" is the real example; any plain spoken equivalent works.
**Hands to AGITATE via:** a shift into the problem, often on a connective
like "And look..."

**Technique — reluctant-hero credentialing.** Credentials arrive INSIDE a
story or a fast list that trails off, never as a standalone resume up
front. The tell is the dismissiveness at the end of the list, not the list
itself.

**Example** (shape only — the real line names this source's own years and
companies, which stay out of this file): *"I've been doing [this work] for
[a long time]. [One credential]. You know, that whole thing."* Then a
second beat of genuine astonishment, real and unhedged: *"And what I
discovered... honestly, I still can't fully wrap my head around it."*

**Honesty rule:** the story is pulled from `brain/stories/`, AS WRITTEN. Do
not invent a founding story, and do not sand off its rough edges to make it
punchier. Any credential stated (years in business, a number, a
qualification) traces to `brain/business.md`'s hard facts or the owner's own
words this session — never invented, never rounded up.

---

## 3. AGITATE — make the status quo cost something

**Share:** ~13% of runtime.
**Job:** Make the current situation specifically unbearable, not
dramatically unbearable. Show you understand what they already tried — this
positions the business as someone who gets it, not someone selling at them.
**Opens with:** "And look, I tried everything before I figured this out" —
or the honest equivalent for this business's own before-story.
**Hands to MECHANISM via:** the pivot into what actually changed — the
epiphany, not a further list of problems.

**Technique — acknowledge the real attempts, specifically.** Naming what
they likely already tried (the obvious first moves, by name, not vaguely)
does more work than a generic description of pain. Specificity is what
earns "yeah, that's me."

**Example** (shape only — the real attempts named are specific to this
source's own market): *"I tried [the obvious first options]. [Attempt
one]. [Attempt two]. And yeah, the output was decent. But it was never
really done."* And a short, quotable cost-of-the-status-quo line, real and
clean: *"You're still the person in between."*

**Honesty rule:** the "already tried this" list and its cost come from
`brain/audience.md`'s objections and pains, or real research (a support
inbox, a sales-call pattern, a review) — never a struggle invented because
it sounds relatable. If real transcripts or an objection log exist in
`brain/research/` or `brain/inbox/`, the most-repeated pains earn the
specifics here; that is the only "ranking" this section needs.

## 4. MECHANISM — the insight, then the demo

**Share:** ~26% of runtime — the single biggest block, wider than PROOF and
OFFER combined.
**Job:** Explain WHY everything else failed and why this works — the
insight, not the product pitch yet. The product shows up as the vehicle for
the insight, not the other way round. A demo or walkthrough sits inside this
block, not as a separate step.
**Opens with:** a specific narrative scene, told in the past tense — a
moment, not a summary.
**Hands to PROOF via:** a pivot word — "Now..." — into evidence the insight
is real.

**Technique — the Big Domino.** One contrarian reframe, argued once, fully.
A VSL that argues three separate value props loses conversion arguing all
three halfway; MECHANISM exists to knock down exactly one domino. Resist
adding a second argument here because it is also true — true and relevant
is not the same as belongs in this video.

**Technique — objections as loops, never a standalone Q&A.** Where an
objection needs answering, weave it as: the belief -> why it's wrong -> what
is actually true -> the proof that backs it. It should read as part of the
explanation, not as a "you might be thinking..." block bolted on. There is
no separate Objection Handling section in this structure — if you find
yourself writing one, fold it back in here.

**Technique — a pillar summary, stated once.** If the mechanism breaks into
a few named parts, a short rule-of-three summary in parallel verbs, stated
ONCE as a structural device, is a legitimate close to this section — the
illustrative shape is *"[Verb]. [Verb]. [Verb]."* This differs from
sprinkling three-item lists at the sentence level, which is the AI tell the
Editor gate flags. One rhythmic triad, once, as a mnemonic — not a habit.

**Example** (shape only — the real line names this source's own specific
product context): *"I did the opposite. Instead of [redoing the output by
hand every time], I built the [process/system] that produces it."* This
"fix the process, not the output" shape is the reusable Big Idea underneath:
old way, redo the work by hand each time; new way, build the thing that does
it on repeat. It fits any operator or builder offer well; it is not the only
Big Idea this skill allows, just the cleanest real example of what a Big
Domino looks like.

**Honesty rule:** the mechanism's content — the steps, the named ideas, the
beliefs — comes from `brain/methodology.md`. A demo describes something
real and available, never a feature that does not exist yet. An objection's
"what is actually true" answer traces to the business's real practice, not
a plausible-sounding explanation invented to fill the loop.

## 5. PROOF — earn it, don't just claim it

**Share:** ~10% of runtime.
**Job:** Show the insight is real, weaving proof types rather than stacking
them in a list: results, authority, a demonstration, something taught in
passing. Own results carry the section fine on their own if that is all
that is cleared to use.
**Opens with:** a pivot or contrast word — "Now, I'm not just selling this"
or the honest equivalent.
**Hands to OFFER via:** a turn from "it works" to "here's what it costs to
get it."

**Technique — contrast over scale.** A small, honest input next to a
disproportionate output does more work than a big raw number alone. "With
not much, this happened" beats "this happened," even when the second number
is bigger in isolation.

**Example** (shape only — the real line carries this source's own account
size and view counts, which stay out of this file): *"With [a modest
number] on [a channel], [one specific piece of output] reached [a much
larger number]."* And the humble frame that should sit near any own-results
claim: *"I genuinely believe [the honest outcome]. And I'm not saying that
to brag."*

**Honesty rule:** the strictest rule in the whole script. Every claim here
comes from `brain/proof/`, verbatim, and only from an entry whose
`approval` field says `approved` — a `pending` entry is not cleared to use,
whatever it says. No customer name, number, or quote ships without that
clearance. Missing proof is `[PLACEHOLDER: what's missing]`, not a reason to
reach for an unapproved entry or invent a plausible number. A thin PROOF
section built honestly beats a strong one that isn't true.

## 6. OFFER — the stack

**Share:** ~13% of runtime.
**Job:** A Brunson-style stack: establish what this should cost, list
what's included, reveal the actual price, justify the gap, and let bonuses
pre-answer the secondary objections that are left.
**Opens with:** "So here's what I built" — mirroring CONNECT's own opening
bridge.
**Hands to CLOSE via:** the price is on the table now; CLOSE decides who it
is actually for.

**Technique — anchor before you reveal.** Name what the alternatives cost
first (an agency, a freelancer, doing it themselves badly) so the real
price lands as a gap, not a number in isolation.

**Example** (shape only, deliberately — the real line is entirely this
source's own prices and product name): *"[Alternative A] costs [amount] a
[period]. [Alternative B] costs [amount] for one [unit]. [This offer] is
[the price structure]."*

**Technique — the launch/evergreen fork.** Rather than writing two scripts,
fork only this section's pricing paragraph. Mark it exactly like this in the
annotated master when both versions are needed:

```text
>>> LAUNCH VERSION (record this) <<<
[the real, owner-stated deadline or cap, and the price that goes with it]

>>> EVERGREEN VERSION (re-record this section only) <<<
[the standing price, no pressure language]
```

Everything else in the script stays one shared recording; only this
paragraph gets a second take later.

**Honesty rule:** price, what's included, AND every operational claim about
how the service actually runs — who picks what, how often, what happens
when — trace to `brain/business.md` (or `methodology.md`). Not stated
there means `[PLACEHOLDER: ...]`, never a plausible guess; and if a
sibling page item already flags that exact fact as an open owner question,
it is open here too. A deadline or a cap in the LAUNCH VERSION is real and
owner-stated — never inferred, never manufactured to create pressure. No real deadline exists:
skip the fork, write the evergreen version only. Bonuses are things that
actually exist and actually ship, not invented sweeteners. If
`brain/compliance.md` exists, check it before writing the price and the
terms.

## 7. CLOSE — qualify, guarantee, future-pace, ask

**Share:** ~16% of runtime.
**Job:** Not one beat — a short sequence inside one section: qualify out
the wrong buyer, state the guarantee, paint the future-paced picture, ask
once, sign off warm. No standalone Objection Handling, Guarantee, or FAQ
block anywhere in this structure — all of it lives here, or, for
objections, back in MECHANISM.
**Opens with:** "Look, I'm not going to pretend this is for everyone" —
echoing AGITATE's "And look" — permission to say no, stated before the ask.
**Hands to:** nothing. This is the end. One CTA, stated once, soft.

**Technique — qualify out by name.** Naming who this is NOT for,
specifically, does more for the right buyer's trust than any amount of
persuading. It also lowers refunds and support load from people who were
never going to be happy.

**Example** (shape only — the real line names this source's own precondition
and product): *"If [the situation where this genuinely will not work], this
isn't for you. [This offer] can't [do the thing] if [the precondition]
isn't true."*

**Technique — future-pace the transformation, not the product.** The
picture painted is what changes for THEM, stated as a contrast with today —
not a features recap. One real, clean example of the shape (this is the
payoff line of THIS source's specific Big Idea, fix-the-process — it will
not fit every business; find the payoff line for whatever Big Idea
MECHANISM actually argued): *"Nothing changed. You just stopped being the
bottleneck."*

**Technique — one CTA, soft, once.** A single clear instruction, not
repeated urgency language. The permission-to-say-no beat comes BEFORE the
ask, not after — it should never read as a trick to lower defenses right
before a hard sell.

**Honesty rule:** who this is NOT for comes from `brain/audience.md`'s own
"Who this is NOT for" line where it exists, plus what's genuinely true
about the offer's fit — never a softened version designed to exclude
nobody. Guarantee terms trace to `brain/business.md` and, where it exists,
`brain/compliance.md`. The future-pace is the real promise from MECHANISM
restated, not a new claim invented for the close.

---

## Structure Invisibility

Read the finished script with every section label covered. If you can still
tell where CAPTURE ends and CONNECT begins from the writing alone — a
visible seam, a "now let's talk about," a tonal jump — smooth it. The seven
parts are a drafting scaffold, never something the listener should be able
to see. This is the single biggest tell that separates a mined structure
from a filled-in template.

## What generalizes, and what must never leave this file

**Carries into every business's script:** the seven-part shape, with
MECHANISM as the deliberate largest block; reluctant-hero credentialing; the
Big Domino rule; objections as woven loops, never a standalone block; three
hooks by rhetorical pattern; the qualify-out close; future-pacing the real
promise; contrast-over-scale proof; the launch/evergreen pricing fork; no em
dash, contractions always, second person singular, grade-8 by default.

**Never carries over:** any name, price, follower count, view count, or
credential detail quoted above as an "Example." Those belong to the business
the canon was mined from. This business's script uses this business's own
real material — from `brain/business.md`, `brain/proof/`, `brain/stories/`,
`brain/methodology.md` — never a number borrowed from an illustration in
this file.
