# Playbook: humanize

The core pass. Remove the machine tells, then put the owner back in. Run inside
the method in SKILL.md (diagnose, plan, apply, audit, final); this file is the
checklist, not a new process.

## 1. Tell removal

Walk `rulebook/tells.md` with the scorer's report open. The scorer detects phrase
patterns across E05-E14 and E22-E25, but detection does not decide severity. E14
remains a judgment finding because the business's source rule controls the remedy.
For other judgment tells (E15-E21, E27-E31), flag only what you would defend out
loud with the quoted text in hand. Severity discipline:

- Hard tells: fix every one, exactly.
- Judgment tells: conservative. When in doubt, leave the sentence alone.
- Invariants (E01-E04): laws. A conflict between a draft claim and the brain is
  resolved by the truth hierarchy in SKILL.md: exact supported wording or a
  verification placeholder, always reported. A missing `[INSERT ...]` asset is
  held and flagged, never silently deleted or filled.

While fixing, keep the register of marketing, not an encyclopedia: a persuasion
piece may keep a list, one bold phrase, an exclamation, a strong stance. These are
carve-outs, not bans. Strip the tic, never the energy.

## 2. Voice restoration

With tells gone, pull the text toward the owner using `brain/voice.md` both ways:

- Produce toward the "Sounds like you" list: their phrases, their rhythm, their
  way in.
- Trip on the "Does not sound like you" list: any word or habit there is a hard
  no for this business.
- Check the "Rules of thumb" (their structural habits: one idea per email, name
  the feeling first, whatever they wrote).
- Match the "Writing samples" above all. Read a sample, read your edit, and ask
  which one the other would recognize. The sample beats any generic rule (E39).

## 3. The personality stage (mandatory, last)

Pattern fixes reach neutral. This stage reaches the actual person. Clean-but-dead
copy fails review, so after the self-audit, deliberately restore bite:

- Vary the rhythm: a four-word sentence next to a thirty-word one.
- Sharpen a position the draft already takes, using the owner's voice. Do not add
  a new position from the brain.
- First person and "you" where it fits their voice.
- Keep and foreground a concrete detail already in the draft. Do not import a
  story, quote, price, name, date, or product detail from the brain for colour.
- Say a feeling specifically, if the voice does that ("this kept me up in March"
  beats "this is concerning").
- Let a little controlled mess live: an aside, a fragment, a question.

Everything in this stage obeys the fact-freeze: source details only. The brain may
verify them or supply exact replacement wording for a direct conflict, never bonus
material. If the draft is thin, say so and stop at clean-and-clear rather than
faking charm.

Pressure is voice; a deadline is a fact. If the input says Friday and the brain
does not contradict it, keep Friday while removing shame, countdown language, or
fake drama around it.

## 4. Variation without costume changes

Different wording on the same paragraph skeleton is not a real variant. For a
multi-version request, assign a different central move to each draft: an existing
moment, the reader's immediate feeling, the plain offer, the mechanism, or proof
already in the input. Then move the source facts around that choice. Vary where the price and
CTA land and how the CTA works. Do not force every fact through the same reveal,
proof, price, reply sequence. Never invent a story to make a version look different.

After drafting the set, count optional repeated moves. Rebuild when more than two
versions share the same CTA words, transition opener family, or final three
paragraph roles. Across a five-version set, all five variants must put proof,
price, and a required placeholder in unique relative orders. A made-up reader action is still a
made-up detail; never use an imagined micro-scene as cheap variation.

Except for byte-locked quotes, placeholders, and an exact conflict correction,
repeat no complete sentence or non-locked claim phrasing in more than two
variants. Rebuild a repeated proof-and-outcome stack even when its facts are
mandatory. Different facts are invention; different choreography and sentence
shape are variation.
