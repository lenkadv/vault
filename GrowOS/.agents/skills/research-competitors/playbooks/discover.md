# Playbook: discover

Read `SKILL.md` first. Its law governs everything below: owner-named
competitors are confirmed on naming; discovered candidates are never read
until the owner says yes.

## Start with what the owner already gave you

Before searching for anyone new, check:

- `brain/competitors.md` — names already on file.
- `brain/decisions.md` — a past call that named or ruled out a competitor
  ("we don't compete with X, they're a different price point").
- This conversation, if running standalone, or a `competitor` / `confirmed`
  entry in the newest source manifest in `brain/research/`, if dispatched.

Only these count as confirmed. Read their sites and reviews freely, same as a
domain-linked audience source. A name that only shows up in `brain/inbox/`,
`add-to-brain/`, a gathered page, or loose notes is not confirmed by sitting
there — list it as a candidate below, same as anything freshly discovered.

## Find the rest

Only when the owner has not already named enough to work with (fewer than two
or three real competitors on file), go looking. Three approaches, use what
fits the business:

1. **Category search.** "[what the business does] near [their area]" for a
   local business, "[category] software" or "[category] tool" for SaaS,
   "[product type] brand" for e-commerce.
2. **"Alternative to [business name]" search.** Sites and forums built exactly
   for this comparison often surface the businesses a real buyer actually
   weighs against this one.
3. **Review-site categories.** G2 and Capterra list software by category; Yelp
   and Google Maps list local businesses by category; app stores list by
   category. The category page itself is a fast way to see who else is
   competing for the same search.

Every name this turns up is a **candidate**, not yet a competitor. Do not open
their site, do not read their reviews, do not write anything about them into
the dossier as fact. List the name and where you found it.

## Confirming candidates

- **Dispatched during onboarding:** the confirmation already happened
  upstream, in its own separately labeled multi-select — "which of these do
  your customers actually compare you with?" — not the "which of these are
  yours?" question, which confirms audience sources instead. Read the newest
  `*-sources.md` manifest in `brain/research/` and treat only its
  `competitor` / `confirmed` entries as confirmed. A name you found that is
  not in the manifest, or sits there `unconfirmed`, is still a candidate:
  list it for a later pass.
- **Standalone:** ask the owner directly, in plain words: "I found a few
  businesses that look like they might compete with you: [names]. Are these
  ones you actually compete with, or are they a different kind of business?"
  One question, plain, no jargon. Write their answer into the manifest the
  same way onboarding does (or start one if none exists).

## Picking the top two or three for Quick

When more than two or three real competitors are confirmed, Quick depth
sketches the ones the owner is most likely to actually be weighed against:
prefer the ones closest in price, in what they offer, and in who they serve.
Say in the dossier which ones you picked and, briefly, why those over the
others. In-depth works through all of them.

## When nothing is confirmed yet

No competitors named, and no web access to go looking, or the owner has not
yet answered a confirmation question: say so in one line, and write the
smallest honest dossier you can — what you looked for, and that the file
stays thin until a competitor is confirmed. A dossier with nobody in it, said
plainly, is more useful than one padded with an unconfirmed guess.
