# Type library

Four types cover every cross-channel campaign. Pick one in Phase 0, before
the interview's other questions - it decides which phase shape and asset
menu the rest of the brief gets built against.

Every number in this file - a phase length, an asset count - is a DEFAULT
the owner adjusts, never a rule to enforce. State the default, then adjust
it to what this business and this campaign actually need.

## Picking a type

| What's in front of you | Type |
|---|---|
| A new offer, product, or tier reaches the world for the first time, or a relaunch big enough to earn its own belief-building runway | `launch` |
| An existing offer at a temporary price, bonus, or bundle, with a real end date or a real cap | `promo-sale` |
| An offer that sells all the time, no real deadline, refreshed on a steady rotation | `evergreen-push` |
| A live teaching or selling event the reader registers for, with a replay after | `webinar` |

Two tie-breaks worth knowing:

- **A launch that centers on a live event is still a launch, not a
  webinar.** Webinar is for when the live event IS the whole campaign -
  registration is the ask, not a side door into something bigger. When a
  launch happens to use a live session as its biggest moment, pick
  `launch`, and just borrow the webinar type's registration / show-up /
  replay-close shape for that one phase's asset menu.
- **No real deadline anywhere in sight is a sign this is `evergreen-push`,
  not `launch` or `promo-sale`.** Say so plainly rather than inventing a
  close date to make the arc feel more like a launch.

## The phase arc (the shape under `launch` and `promo-sale`)

Every launch and promo-sale is a version of the same five-phase arc. Not
every phase always appears - a promo-sale usually skips the event phase
entirely - and the length of each phase is a default the owner adjusts,
never a fixed rule.

1. **Runway** - belief seeding, no offer named yet. The reader does not
   know this is coming. Job: plant the problem or the idea, honestly,
   before asking for anything.
2. **Open** - the offer is named plainly. The reader now knows what this is
   and roughly what it costs them - money, time, or attention.
3. **Event / main push** - the single biggest moment of the campaign, if
   there is one: a live session, a reveal, the peak day of a sale. Not
   every campaign has one - a promo-sale often has no event at all, just
   open running straight into close.
4. **Cart / close** - the real deadline, only when one genuinely exists.
   This phase does not exist if there is no real reason for it to; see the
   real-scarcity rule in `SKILL.md`.
5. **Follow-through** - what happens right after: thank the people who
   acted, capture what happened as proof for later, and go quiet on the
   pitch.

## 1. Launch

What it is: a new offer, product, or tier's first real push, or a relaunch
treated as its own event. The full five-phase arc applies. This is usually
the type with the longest message ladder, because it has to move a reader
the farthest - from not knowing this exists to buying it.

Default relative timeline (the owner's real dates replace these):

| Phase | Default length | What has to be true by the end |
|---|---|---|
| Runway | 3-7 days before open | The idea has been planted at least once on every channel in the channel plan |
| Open | 1-3 weeks | The offer is named; the reader knows what it is and who it is for |
| Event / main push | 1-3 days, if there is one | The reader has seen the thing work, live or as close to live as honest |
| Cart / close | 3-10 days, only if a real deadline exists | The real deadline has been stated to the reader more than once |
| Follow-through | 1-4 weeks after | What happened is captured as proof, in the business's own words |

Per-phase asset menu (typical counts, always a range - the real count comes
from the channel plan, not this table):

- Runway: 1-3 emails, 0-2 social posts, 0-1 video
- Open: 3-8 emails, 3-10 social posts, 0-1 lead magnet, 1-2 pages, 2-6 ad
  creatives, 0-1 video
- Event / main push: the event build-out itself (a webinar build-out, or a
  live-session run-of-show), plus 1-3 supporting emails
- Cart / close: 3-8 emails, escalating, one job each - never restate
  yesterday's email; 0-2 retargeting ad creatives; 0-2 social posts
- Follow-through: 1-2 emails; proof captured for future use, not published
  as part of this campaign's own ask

Channel roles, as a starting point (the channel plan in the brief sets the
real one):

- **Email carries the argument** - it is where the message ladder actually
  climbs, step by step.
- **Social and video build belief** - proof, behind-the-scenes, teaching.
  They earn trust; they rarely close.
- **Ads convert cold traffic to the entry step** - point ads at the
  cheapest, lowest-commitment next action (an opt-in, a registration),
  never straight at the biggest offer.
- **Pages catch** - wherever a click has to land and do its one job:
  register, opt in, or buy.

What "done" looks like: the close date, if any, has passed; every asset
row is accounted for (queued, or a stated reason it never ran); a
follow-through asset has captured what actually happened for future proof.

## 2. Promo-sale

What it is: an existing offer at a temporary price, bonus, or bundle. No
new product reveal, no event phase - the compressed arc is the whole
point. The runway is often short or skipped entirely; the sale itself IS
the open phase.

Default relative timeline:

| Phase | Default length | What has to be true by the end |
|---|---|---|
| Runway (optional) | 0-3 days | If used at all, a light heads-up that something is coming |
| Open | The sale window itself, commonly 3-10 days | The offer, the price, and the real end date are all stated plainly |
| Cart / close | The last 1-3 days of the same window | The real deadline is stated at least once more, honestly, with no new pressure invented |
| Follow-through | 3-7 days after | Thank the people who acted; the offer's return to normal is stated plainly |

Per-phase asset menu:

- Runway (if used): 0-1 email, 0-1 social post
- Open: 2-5 emails, 2-6 social posts, 1-3 ad creatives, 1 page (or an
  existing page updated)
- Cart / close: 1-3 emails, 0-1 retargeting ad creative
- Follow-through: 1 email

Channel roles: same defaults as launch, compressed - email still carries
the argument, ads still point at the entry step, which here is usually the
sale page itself, since there is rarely a separate cheap first step to
sell.

What "done" looks like: the close date has passed, the page or offer has
been handed back to the owner to revert to normal (this skill does not do
that itself), and one follow-through asset closes the loop.

## 3. Evergreen-push

What it is: an offer that sells all the time, with no real deadline. There
is no cart/close phase, ever - not a soft one, not one kept "for
structure." The timeline section of this type's brief states a standing
cadence instead of a close date.

Default relative timeline: there is not one in the launch/promo-sale
sense. Instead, state a rotation cadence - for example, refresh the ad
creative every 4-6 weeks, or rotate in a new social angle every 2 weeks.
Pick a default review point too (commonly every 4-8 weeks) so the push
gets checked for staleness rather than running forever unexamined.

Per-cycle asset menu (repeats on the cadence above, not a one-time list):

- 1-2 evergreen emails in the nurture or newsletter rotation
- 2-4 social posts per cycle
- 1-3 ad creatives, refreshed on the cadence - fatigue is the trigger to
  refresh, not the calendar alone
- 1 standing page, revisited for accuracy each cycle rather than rebuilt

Channel roles: the same defaults as launch, with one hard difference -
there is no cart/close phase to escalate toward, so nothing in this list
should read like it is building urgency. Ads and pages carry more of the
weight here, since there is no live moment for email to build toward.

What "done" looks like: evergreen-push is never "done" the way a launch
is. "Healthy" means the rotation is current, nothing has gone stale, and
no scarcity language has crept in anywhere. Revisit it on the review
cadence, not only when something breaks.

## 4. Webinar

What it is: a live teaching or selling event the reader registers for,
with a replay after. Its own three-phase shape, not the general five-phase
arc:

| Phase | Default length | What has to be true by the end |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | 1-3 weeks | Registrations are coming in; the reader knows what they get from showing up live |
| Show-up | The 2-3 days around the live date | Reminders have gone out; the live session happens; attendance is real, not assumed |
| Replay-close | 3-7 days after | The replay is available; if a real offer and a real deadline are tied to it, both get stated plainly and the close is honest |

Per-phase asset menu:

- Registration: the registration page/build-out itself, 3-6 emails, 2-5
  social posts, 0-1 video, 1-4 ad creatives - pointed at registration,
  never at a bigger offer
- Show-up: 2-4 reminder emails, the live run-of-show itself
- Replay-close: 2-5 emails, 0-1 retargeting ad creative

Channel roles: ads and social exist here to fill the room - never ask them
to sell the backend offer directly. Email carries both the registration
push and, after the event, whatever real close exists.

What "done" looks like: the replay window has closed, or the business's
usual policy for leaving it up has been confirmed; every
registration/show-up/replay-close row is accounted for; if a real offer
was tied to the event, its close is honestly stated as closed.
