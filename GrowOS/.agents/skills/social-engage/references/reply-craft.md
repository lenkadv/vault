# Reply craft

A reply is not a small post. It answers one specific thing someone already
said, in a space that already belongs to them as much as the business — a
comment thread, a DM inbox. This file covers what changes when the copy
being drafted is a reply instead of a fresh post: length, register, when to
take a public thread private, and the handful of things a reply should
never do regardless of platform.

For the underlying platform norms — what that platform's audience is like,
its native formats, its posting hooks — read
`system/creative-library/platforms/instagram.md`, `facebook.md`,
`linkedin.md`, and `x.md` fresh each time. This file does not restate them;
it only covers what's different about answering something there versus
posting something there.

## Universal reply rules (every platform, every surface)

- **Shorter than a post.** A reply answers or acknowledges; it doesn't
  restate the whole pitch. One sentence is often enough. If a drafted reply
  is running to several sentences with its own hook and its own
  call-to-action, it has started to read like a post wearing a reply's
  clothes — cut it back down.
- **No hashtags, ever, in a reply, on any platform.** A hashtag exists to
  help a fresh post get found by people who don't already follow the
  account. A reply is already sitting inside a conversation someone else
  started — it has nothing to be "found" for, and a hashtag under a comment
  reads as an obvious template the moment anyone sees it.
- **Sparse to no emoji**, unless `brain/voice.md` says otherwise. A reply is
  a smaller, more personal space than a post; one emoji, only when it
  genuinely fits the moment, never as a verbal tic repeated across the
  batch.
- **No sign-offs.** Never "Best," "Talk soon," a name signature, or
  anything else that belongs at the bottom of a letter. Nobody signs a
  comment or a DM in real life — it is one of the fastest tells that a reply
  came from a template instead of a person.
- **No engagement bait tacked onto the end.** "What do you think?" or "Let
  me know!" added just to keep a thread going, with nothing real behind the
  question, reads as hollow. End when the point is made. A real question
  belongs when there's a real reason to ask one — a lead's qualifying
  question, for instance — never as a reflexive close.
- **Answer the actual thing they said.** Reference something specific from
  their message so it's obvious it was actually read, not matched to a
  category and answered with a stock line.
- **Sound like a person replying, not a brand issuing a statement.** Match
  `brain/voice.md`, but expect a reply to sit even more casual than that
  business's own post voice — contractions, plain words, nothing that reads
  like a press release.

## Per platform

### Instagram — comments

Format norms: `system/creative-library/platforms/instagram.md`.

Comments are public and skimmed fast — one or two sentences is usually
enough. Emoji fit a little more naturally here than on most platforms,
matching the platform's own culture, but default to `voice.md`'s own
setting when it says nothing (sparingly, per the universal rule above).

Move the conversation to DM when the real answer needs specifics that
shouldn't sit in public (pricing back-and-forth, anything personal), or the
exchange is heading past two or three replies in the open. Only say "sent
you a DM" inside the public reply when this same batch is actually drafting
that DM too — never promise a DM the batch doesn't include.

### Instagram — DMs

More room to be conversational, and a little longer than a comment, but
still well short of a post — a DM is a conversation, not a pitch delivered
in private.

This is the natural home for a lead's real next step: a link, if one is on
file, a direct question back, an invitation to book. No hashtags here
either, obviously — a DM has no discovery mechanism for a hashtag to serve.

### Facebook — comments

Format norms: `system/creative-library/platforms/facebook.md`.

Similar register to Instagram, skewing a touch more community and local per
the platform file. Public, so keep anything account-specific out of it —
that's the support carve-out's job regardless of platform.

Facebook comment threads can run long and stay very visible. A reply that's
turning into a real back-and-forth objection is a good candidate to take
private, but Facebook's own comment-to-DM culture is less universal a norm
than Instagram's — when it's not obviously the right call, keep it short and
public instead of assuming a DM is welcome.

### LinkedIn — comments

Format norms: `system/creative-library/platforms/linkedin.md`.

The most professional register of the four platforms — still a person, not
a press release, but nothing that would look odd sitting under someone's
real name and job title. Short, substantive, adds one real thought rather
than "Great post!" energy.

A comment that's actually a business inquiry dressed up as a comment
("would love to chat about working together") is exactly the case for
taking it to DM — LinkedIn's own culture expects that move more than any
other platform here.

### LinkedIn — DMs

Reads closer to a short professional email than a text message — still
short, still a real person, but a notch more complete in its sentences than
an Instagram DM would be.

Watch the thread's own history: if this DM is the first message in the
conversation (someone messaged in cold, not replying to anything the
business said), don't refer to a comment or a prior message that never
happened — just answer what they actually wrote.

### X — replies

Format norms: `system/creative-library/platforms/x.md`.

The 280-character limit applies to a reply exactly as it does to a fresh
post — count it before finishing. X's whole culture rewards a reply that's
sharp and fast; don't pad a genuinely one-line answer into three sentences
just to sound complete.

No hashtags, matching the platform's own posting norm. A link is fine in an
X reply when a lead's next step actually calls for one — the platform's own
file already treats a reply as the right place for a link when the main
post can't carry it, so the "links hurt reach" rule for a fresh X post does
not carry over to a reply here.

## DM etiquette (every platform with a DM surface)

- Never open like a form letter — "Thank you for reaching out to us" is the
  opposite of what a DM should sound like. Reply like a person continuing a
  conversation that's already underway.
- Don't repeat their whole message back before answering it. Answer first.
- A DM can carry a link when one is on file; a public comment mostly
  shouldn't — a raw link in a comment reads as spammy, and most platforms
  visually bury it anyway. Prefer "check your DMs" or "it's in our bio" over
  pasting a URL into a public reply, unless the platform and the business's
  own style genuinely make a plain link normal there.
- The support carve-out applies exactly the same in a DM as in a public
  comment. A DM being private doesn't make a refund or order question any
  more this skill's job to answer — flag it, don't draft it, however
  quietly it arrived.

## Voice consistency across every reply in a batch

- **Shorter than the business's own posts.** If a drafted reply could pass
  for a short post — multiple paragraphs, a hook, a call-to-action stacked
  on top — it's too long for what it is. Cut it down.
- **No sign-offs, ever.** Worth restating on its own: this is the single
  most common tell in a drafted reply, and the easiest one to let slip back
  in out of habit.
- **Sounds like a person.** Plain words, contractions where they'd
  naturally fall, first person the way `voice.md` actually has this
  business talk about itself — never marketing language creeping back in
  just because "the business" is technically still the one talking.
- **Consistent within the batch, not uniform across platforms.** Every
  reply in one batch is the same business, the same voice — don't let the
  Instagram reply sound like a different person from the LinkedIn reply.
  What's allowed to shift is formality: LinkedIn sits a notch more
  composed, Instagram and X a notch more casual, but the underlying voice
  from `brain/voice.md` never changes underneath that.
